Gyeongju Food Guide: Ssambap, Sundubu, Kongguk, Bibimbap, Tteokgalbi and Gimbap

Here are the Korean dishes worth trying in Gyeongju. We list dishes people in Gyeongju have eaten for a long time and dishes that are popular around Hwangnidan-gil, together with places to eat them.

We chose the shops by their number of reviews on Naver Map (네이버 지도), a Korean map app. For each dish, we searched Gyeongju shops on Naver Map and picked the ones with the most reviews among shops that serve that dish as a main menu. Each table is also in order of reviews, most first.

Review counts were checked in November 2026. When several shops share a name, as with a chain, only the branch with the most reviews is in the table.

As of November 20, 2026 — opening days and menus can change. Check before you go.

1. Ssambap - a full table of rice and side dishes wrapped in leaves

Ssambap (쌈밥) means wrapping rice, meat and ssamjang (a thick dipping paste) in leaves such as lettuce, napa cabbage or perilla. Ssambap restaurants in Gyeongju usually serve it as a set meal: the leaves come with grilled fish, bulgogi, doenjang-jjigae (soybean paste stew) and many side dishes on one table.

The City of Gyeongju introduces the area around Daereungwon (대릉원) as a ssambap street. It is an easy meal after visiting the historic sites, so ssambap has long been a standard meal on a Gyeongju trip.

Photo: Ureong Gaksi (우렁각시), 128-1 Ha-dong, Gyeongju-si, Gyeongbuk (경북 경주시 하동 128-1)

To make a wrap, spread a leaf on your palm, add a little rice and meat, top it with ssamjang and eat it in one bite. A big wrap is hard to fit in your mouth, so keep it small.

Ssambap near Daereungwon
Good stops on a day you walk around Cheomseongdae (첨성대), Daereungwon and Hwangnidan-gil (황리단길).

2. Sundubu - soft tofu made fresh from soybeans every day

Regular tofu is made by grinding soybeans into soy milk, adding a coagulant such as nigari so it curdles, then pouring it into a mold and pressing out the water until it is firm. You can cut it into neat blocks with a knife.

Sundubu (순두부, soft tofu) is the same soy milk curdled but never pressed. It holds a lot of water and is soft enough to eat with a spoon. Supermarkets sell it in tube-shaped plastic packs.

Sundubu restaurants cook this soft tofu in a ttukbaegi (an earthenware pot). The most common dish is sundubu-jjigae, a red, spicy soft tofu stew. Some places also serve white sundubu boiled without chili seasoning. With white sundubu, you add the seasoning sauce yourself to taste.

Photo: Jeontong Maetdol Sundubu (전통맷돌순두부), 103 Bomun-dong, Gyeongju-si, Gyeongbuk (경북 경주시 보문동 103)

A ttukbaegi (뚝배기) is a clay bowl that doubles as a cooking pot. It goes from the fire straight to your table, so the food keeps bubbling for a while after it arrives.

That is why broth that bubbles over dries on the rim and leaves marks. It may look messy, but it shows the dish was just cooked. The pot is very hot, so do not touch it with bare hands.

Sundubu restaurants cluster along the road into Bomun Tourist Complex (보문관광단지), in Bukgun-dong and on Supmeori-gil. Some open early, so they are a good breakfast spot if you stay in Bomun.

Maetdol Sundubu opened in 1994. It says it grinds soybeans grown in Korea with a millstone to make sundubu every day. It opens at 8 a.m. and is closed on Thursdays.

3. Kongguk - warm soy milk with glutinous rice doughnuts for breakfast

Kongguk (콩국) is soy milk made from ground soybeans and served warm. It is the same soy milk used for kongguksu, the cold noodle dish eaten in summer, but in Gyeongju kongguk is served warm with things like sesame, honey and chapssal doughnuts (glutinous rice doughnuts) and eaten for breakfast. It is similar to Chinese doujiang (豆漿).

Gyeongju Wonjo Kongguk is said to have started in 1956, when the owner, who ran a tofu factory, shared the water from boiled soybeans with neighbors. There are three kinds of warm kongguk depending on what goes in. The signature one comes with black sesame, black beans, honey and chapssal doughnuts.

It opens at 9 a.m. and is closed on Sundays. It also has other soybean dishes such as kongguksu and sundubu-jjigae.

4. Cockle bibimbap and yukhoe bibimbap - bibimbap in the Hwangnidan-gil alleys

Bibimbap (비빔밥) is rice topped with vegetables, meat and seasoning, mixed together before eating. Several Korean restaurants serving bibimbap stand in the alleys west of Daereungwon and around Hwangnidan-gil.

Kkomak bibimbap (꼬막비빔밥, cockle bibimbap) is made with kkomak, small cockles seasoned in soy sauce, mixed with rice. At Hyanghwajeong, the seasoned cockles and rice come half and half on one plate, as a dish for two.

Photo: Hyanghwajeong (향화정), 7 Sajeong-dong, Gyeongju-si, Gyeongbuk (경북 경주시 사정동 7)

Yukhoe bibimbap (육회비빔밥) is bibimbap topped with seasoned raw beef (yukhoe) and vegetables. If you would rather not eat raw meat, pick a dish with cooked meat, such as the seasoned beef rib bibimbap at Yeomion.

Photo: Yeomion (여미온), 224-4 Hwangnam-dong, Gyeongju-si, Gyeongbuk (경북 경주시 황남동 224-4)

Bibimbap places on these streets also serve dishes that go well with bibimbap. Hyanghwajeong has yukhoe mulhoe (raw beef in cold broth) and seafood pancake, and Sohyangmong has a whole-shrimp minari (water parsley) pancake and noodles. In a group, order a few and share.

5. Tteokgalbi - grilled patties of minced short rib

Tteokgalbi (떡갈비) is short rib meat finely minced, seasoned, shaped into flat patties and grilled. There are no bones to pick around, so it is easy for children and anyone who prefers meat in small bites.

It tastes similar to bulgogi grilled over charcoal. The soy sauce marinade is sweet and salty, and because the meat is minced, it is tender to chew.

Reviews by foreign travelers also describe it as tender and juicy with a sweet and salty marinade. Some say it goes well with rice, and others enjoy wrapping it in lettuce.

Bomun-tteul is a Korean restaurant near Bomun Tourist Complex. Its main dishes are galbi-jjim (braised beef short ribs), braised pork ribs and yukhoe bibimbap, and tteokgalbi made from hanu (Korean beef) is also on the menu.

Photo: Bomun-tteul (보문뜰), 40-27 Bomun-dong, Gyeongju-si, Gyeongbuk (경북 경주시 보문동 40-27)

Wonjo Tteokgalbi is a tteokgalbi restaurant on the way to Bulguksa. The tteokgalbi set comes with rice, doenjang-jjigae and side dishes, and there is also a set with yeonnip-bap, rice steamed in a lotus leaf. It opens in the morning, so it suits a day when you visit Bulguksa early.

Photo: Wonjo Tteokgalbi (원조떡갈비), 317-3 Ma-dong, Gyeongju-si, Gyeongbuk (경북 경주시 마동 317-3)

6. Gimbap - Gyori gimbap packed with egg strips, and burdock gimbap

Gimbap (김밥) is rice and fillings rolled up in dried seaweed (gim) and sliced. Gyeongju has two well-known kinds of gimbap.

Gyori gimbap (교리김밥) started near Gyochon Village (교촌마을). Instead of thick egg strips, it is packed with thin, noodle-like shreds of egg crepe, so the slices look yellow inside and feel fluffy to bite.

Burdock gimbap (우엉김밥) is filled with plenty of burdock root braised in soy sauce. Burdock gimbap from Seongdong Market (성동시장) in Gyeongju became famous, and now there is a burdock gimbap shop on Hwangnidan-gil too.

Besides gimbap, the Gyori Gimbap main shop serves Gyori guksu, noodles topped with egg crepe strips. Hwangnam Ueong Gimbap offers takeout.
