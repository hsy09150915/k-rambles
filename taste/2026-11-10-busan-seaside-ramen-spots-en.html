Where to Eat Ramen by the Sea in Busan: Convenience Store Ramen Machines and Seafood Ramyeon

Some convenience stores in Korea have an instant ramen machine, so you can buy a packet of ramyeon and cook it on the spot. In Busan there are several places where you can eat ramen from one of these machines while looking at the sea.

This guide covers convenience stores and ramen cafes where you cook it yourself, and seaside restaurants that cook haemul-ramyeon (seafood ramyeon) for you. It also explains how to use the machine.

As of November 10, 2026 - Opening hours and machines can change. Check a map app before you go.

0. Before you go - Hangang ramyeon and the instant ramen machine

Cooking ramen at a convenience store in Seoul's Hangang Park and eating it by the river is commonly called Hangang ramyeon (한강라면) in Korea. It shows up a lot in Korean dramas and on social media, so it has become famous with foreign travelers too.

The thing you use for it is an instant ramen machine. You put a packet of ramen in a paper bowl, set it on the machine and press a button. The machine pours in the right amount of hot water and boils it for a set time.

Not every convenience store has one. Almost every store has a hot water dispenser for cup noodles, but the machine that boils packet ramen is only in some stores. The two look different. The dispenser only gives hot water, while the instant ramen machine adds the water and boils the ramen itself.

Busan has the sea instead of the Hangang River. Below are places where you can cook ramen with this machine and eat it with a view of the sea.

1. How to use an instant ramen machine

The machines look a little different by brand, but the steps are almost the same. There is a picture guide next to the machine, and some stores have instructions in several languages.

StepWhat to do
1. Pick your ramenChoose a packet of ramen from the shelf. You will see familiar ones such as Shin Ramyun, Jin Ramen, Neoguri and Chapagetti.
2. Pay with a bowlYou need to buy the special paper bowl separately from the ramen. Pay for both together at the counter or a self-checkout machine.
3. Fill the bowlPut the noodles and soup powder in the bowl. If you want egg or cheese, buy them at the same time.
4. Set it on the machinePlace the bowl on the machine and press the button for your type of ramen. Water comes out and it starts to boil. The buttons are usually split into regular ramen, jjamppong (spicy seafood) ramen, stir-fried ramen and jjajang (black bean) ramen.
5. Take it out and eatThe machine stops when it is done. Chopsticks are at the counter or next to the machine.

Some stores have different bowls for different types of ramen. If there are separate bowls for regular ramen, thick noodles and stir-fried noodles like Chapagetti, check the note next to the price tag on the shelf and pick the right one.

Add a raw egg while the ramen is boiling. Some stores have a note saying that an egg added before it boils can stick to the bottom of the bowl.

If you like your noodles firm, you can press the stop button and take it out before it finishes.

Do not put cup noodles on this machine. It only works with the special bowl.

GS25 Songjeong Premium - right in front of Songjeong Beach, 2nd floor window seats

This two-story convenience store is across the beach road from Songjeong Beach (송정해수욕장). You shop on the 1st floor, and on the 2nd floor one wall is filled with ramen and there are several instant ramen machines.

You can see the Songjeong sea from the 2nd floor window seats. Many people drop in after swimming or surfing.

CU Millak Waterfront Park - in front of the park with a view of Gwangan Bridge

This store is east of Gwangalli Beach, right in front of Millak Waterfront Park (민락수변공원). Ask for a bowl with your packet of ramen when you pay, and you can cook and eat it in the store.

You can also carry your ramen across the road to Millak Waterfront Park and eat it while looking at Gwangan Bridge. The park has rows of step-like seating facing the sea, and white canopies that keep off sun and rain, so you can eat under them.

If you are taking it to the park, press the stop button before the machine finishes. The noodles keep cooking in the soup while you walk, so if you carry it fully cooked, they go soggy fast.

Millak Waterfront Park has been an alcohol-free zone since 2023, and drinking there can get you a fine. Eating food is fine.

CU The Ssangyong Osiria - a ramen library by the sea

This convenience store is on the 1st floor of the shopping block of a seaside apartment complex in Yeonhwa-ri (연화리), Gijang. The store is much bigger than a regular CU and has a ramen library (라면도서관) where ramen is displayed like books in a library. You enter the ramen library through the 1st floor store.

It is open 24 hours. The sea is right in front of the store, so you can see the Yeonhwa-ri sea from the windows.

The ramen library that used to be at CU Yeonhwa-ri Ssangyong Center has recently turned into a regular convenience store. If you go by the old name in a map app, there is no ramen library there, so be sure to search for CU The Ssangyong Osiria (CU 더쌍용오시리아점).

7-Eleven Busan Gijang Yeonhwa-ri - the pink store in front of the seafood tent street

This 7-Eleven with a pink exterior is right in front of the seafood pojangmacha (tent restaurant) street in Yeonhwa-ri. It has an instant ramen machine and is open 24 hours.

In front of the store you can see the Yeonhwa-ri sea and the small island of Jukdo. It is a nice spot to finish with a bowl of ramen after eating seafood at the haenyeo (women divers) village.

The two stores are a few minutes apart on foot, so they are easy to combine with the Osiria area, home to Haedong Yonggungsa Temple and Lotte World Adventure Busan. Also see the east coast course from Songjeong to Haedong Yonggungsa.

3. Convenience store gimbap to go with your ramen

Gimbap goes well with ramen. Korean convenience stores now sell many kinds of long whole-roll gimbap like the ones at bunsik-jip (snack shops). Plain gimbap, tuna gimbap and bulgogi gimbap are the basics, and new ones made with popular restaurants keep coming out.

Koreans eat a lot of convenience store gimbap too. As eating out has become more expensive, more office workers have lunch at convenience stores, and GS25 said its gimbap sales in fall 2026 were up 36% from a year earlier. That is why the gimbap shelf, full in the morning, empties quickly as lunchtime gets close.

It costs less than gimbap at a snack shop, yet comes in many flavors and the quality is very high. It is popular with foreigners as well: at CU, bulgogi gimbap was the No. 2 item bought by foreign customers in 2025.

Convenience store gimbap is sold chilled. You can eat it as it is, but it tastes best warmed up a little in the microwave. Even the same tuna gimbap has a different amount of filling at each chain, so trying several brands is fun too.

Whole-roll gimbap on a convenience store fridge shelf. There are many kinds, such as bulgogi, tuna and soy sauce chicken.

4. Self-service ramen cafes by the sea

There are also self-service ramen cafes that sell only ramen. There is no staff: you pay at a kiosk and cook it yourself on an instant ramen machine. They usually have more kinds of ramen and more seats than a convenience store.

Cheongsapo Bada Ramyeon Cafe - open 24 hours, lighthouse views from the rooftop

It is on the 4th floor of a seaside building in Cheongsapo. Pick your ramen, pay at the counter, and cook it at whichever of the four instant ramen machines is free. Instructions for the machines are posted in several languages.

Through the big windows you can see the Cheongsapo lighthouses and the sea, and there are also seats on the rooftop upstairs. There are seats for one and rooms for groups.

Photo: Cheongsapo Bada Ramyeon Cafe (570 Jung-dong, Haeundae-gu, Busan)

Songjeong Bada Ramyeon Cafe - an outdoor terrace where you can hear the waves

It is at Gudeokpo (구덕포), the south end of Songjeong Beach. Pay for ramen and toppings at the kiosk, cook it on the machine, then go out the door to the terrace and eat looking at the sea.

Outside there are raised wooden platforms with triangle roofs and wooden tables, and the lights come on at night.

It is open at night too, and some reviews say you can bring your dog inside. The sea wind is strong, so bring a jacket at night.

Photo: Songjeong Bada Ramyeon Cafe (799-21 Songjeong-dong, Haeundae-gu, Busan)

Huinnyeoul Ramyeon - window seats in Huinnyeoul Culture Village, Yeongdo

This self-service ramen shop is in Huinnyeoul Culture Village (흰여울문화마을), a cliffside village right above the sea. There are seats along the windows facing the sea, plus round tables.

The bowl is included in the price of the ramen, so you just pay and put it straight on the machine. It opens at 8 a.m. and closes in the evening. It is not open 24 hours.

5. Seaside restaurants that cook ramen for you

If cooking it yourself sounds like a hassle, some places cook it for you when you order. Seaside restaurants often sell haemul-ramyeon (해물라면, seafood ramyeon) with seafood such as blue crab, shrimp, mussels and squid.

Some are snack shops where ramen is the star, and some are restaurants where people order seafood ramyeon to finish after raw fish or grilled shellfish. The table below lists both kinds.

Huinnyeoul Jeomppang - a bowl of ramen by the window in Huinnyeoul Culture Village

A small snack shop in an alley of Huinnyeoul Culture Village. It has a basic ramen with Busan fish cake, mushrooms and a boiled egg, and a seafood ramyeon with blue crab, scallops, shrimp and squid. It also sells toast and drinks.

You order on the 1st floor and pick up your food yourself when your number is called. You also return your dishes yourself. Each person must order one menu item.

You can see the sea from the window seats, but there are only a few of them, and the stairs are narrow and steep. There may be a wait at lunchtime.

Ramen in yellow aluminum pots at a window seat in Huinnyeoul Jeomppang. Because of copyright, this image was rebuilt from a real photo of the store to look exactly like the real place.

Okcheon Hoetjip - octopus seafood ramyeon on a deck by the Jungni sea

A long-running raw fish restaurant at the entrance down to the Jungni (중리) haenyeo village on the east side of Yeongdo. It is known for nakji haemul-ramyeon (낙지해물라면, seafood ramyeon with small octopus and big mussels) and small rolls of sea urchin gimbap (성게김밥).

There are many outdoor deck seats facing the sea, as well as indoor seats. From the deck you can see the red lighthouse at the end of the breakwater. You can walk down from Jungni Sunset Observatory.

It is closed every Tuesday, with a break from 3 to 4 p.m. It is open until 9 p.m.

Photo: Okcheon Hoetjip (640-2 Dongsam-dong, Yeongdo-gu, Busan)

Dadaepo Halmae-jip - seaside outdoor tables loved by locals in western Busan

A restaurant with a pojangmacha (tent restaurant) feel, run by the same family for three generations on the shore next to Dadaepo Beach. It is not a ramen restaurant, but it is introduced here as one of the places locals in western Busan love.

The main menu is food to go with drinks, such as muneo-samhap (octopus with pork and kimchi), jeon (Korean pancakes) and haemul-eomuktang (seafood and fish cake soup), and they also have homemade makgeolli (rice wine). Seafood ramyeon is one of many items on the menu, and many people order it to finish after the other dishes.

The outdoor seats facing the sea and the rooftop seats are popular, so the wait gets long around sunset. Once the waiting list closes, you cannot sit outside or on the rooftop.

It opens at 4 p.m. on weekdays and at noon on weekends, and is closed on Mondays. Dadaepo is where the sun sets into the sea in Busan, so it is good to time your visit for sunset.

Photo: Dadaepo Halmae-jip (468-42 Dadae-dong, Saha-gu, Busan)

Mipo Kkeutjip - seaside outdoor tables in Mipo with a view of Gwangan Bridge

An outdoor restaurant on the Mipo (미포) shore at the east end of Haeundae Beach. It is not a ramen restaurant, but it is introduced here because many people go for the outdoor seaside atmosphere.

The main menu is jogae-gui (grilled shellfish), cooked over a coal briquette fire with onion and butter, and they also sell grilled eel. Seafood ramyeon is one of many items on the menu, and many people order it to finish after the grilled shellfish.

From the outdoor seats right by the sea you can see Gwangan Bridge in the distance.

Photo: Mipo Kkeutjip (994-3 Jung-dong, Haeundae-gu, Busan)

Songjeong Haenyeo-jip, Gijang Osiria - abalone seafood ramyeon by the window

A seafood restaurant on the Osiria coast in Gijang. Along with a platter of seafood caught by haenyeo (women divers), it sells jeonbok haemul-ramyeon (전복해물라면), a seafood ramyeon with abalone, octopus, shrimp and scallops.

You can see the sea from the window seats, but even with a reservation you cannot choose a window seat. Seats are given in order of arrival. It is closed every Tuesday.

Photo: Songjeong Haenyeo-jip, Gijang Osiria (540-5 Sirang-ri, Gijang-eup, Gijang-gun, Busan)

7. What to do after you eat

Pour leftover soup in the right place. Convenience stores and ramen cafes have a soup bin or sink. Do not throw the bowl into the trash with soup in it.

Sort the bowl and chopsticks. Put paper bowls, plastic wrappers and wooden chopsticks in separate recycling bins.

If you ate outside, take your trash back. Bring bowls you took to the beach or park back to the store, or put them in a nearby recycling bin.

Self-service shops are watched by CCTV. Clean up your seat before you leave.
