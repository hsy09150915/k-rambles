Gyeongju Meat Dishes: Galbi-jjim, Deunggalbi-jjim, Hanwoo BBQ, Samgyeopsal and Bulgogi

Here are the meat dishes worth trying in Gyeongju. From the galbi-jjim and deunggalbi-jjim that people eat around Hwangnidan-gil (황리단길) to hanwoo (Korean beef) BBQ, charcoal-grilled pork and wire-grilled bulgogi, we list each dish together with places to eat it.

We chose the shops by their number of reviews on Naver Map (네이버 지도), a Korean map app. For each dish, we searched Gyeongju shops on Naver Map and picked the ones with the most reviews among shops that serve that dish as a main menu. Each table is also in order of reviews, most first.

Review counts were checked in November 2026. When several shops share a name, as with a chain, only the branch with the most reviews is in the table.

As of November 25, 2026 — opening days and menus can change. Check before you go.

1. Galbi-jjim - beef short ribs braised slowly in seasoning

Galbi-jjim (갈비찜) is beef short ribs cooked for a long time in seasoning until only a little sauce is left. The long cooking makes the meat so tender that it comes off the bone easily.

Jjim (찜) is defined by the Standard Korean Language Dictionary of the National Institute of Korean Language as meat or vegetables seasoned and boiled or steamed until only a little liquid remains. Unlike tang (soup) or jjigae (stew), which you eat by the spoonful, you put the meat and sauce on rice.

Geosong Galbijjim (거송갈비찜) Spicy galbi-jjim served with flat fried dumplings
Photo: Geosong Galbijjim (거송갈비찜), 227-5 Hwangnam-dong, Gyeongju-si, Gyeongbuk (경북 경주시 황남동 227-5)

Many galbi-jjim shops on Hwangnidan-gil serve it not only in soy sauce seasoning but also in gochujang (red chili paste) or spicy seasoning. Some shops let you choose the spice level.

Many Google Maps reviews say the meat falls softly off the bone. Some reviewers who are used to spicy food still found the spicy version quite hot, so on your first visit choose the soy sauce version or the mildest level.

Many shops sell galbi-jjim for 2 people or more. If you are alone, ask whether they serve a single portion when you order.

2. Deunggalbi-jjim - spicy braised pork back ribs

Deunggalbi-jjim (등갈비찜) is pork back ribs braised with spicy seasoning or mugeunji (long-aged kimchi). It is often spicier than beef galbi-jjim.

Pungmi Galbijjim (풍미갈비찜)'s deunggalbi-jjim
Photo: Pungmi Galbijjim (풍미갈비찜), 228-3 Hwangnam-dong, Gyeongju-si, Gyeongbuk (경북 경주시 황남동 228-3)

At Hwangnam Dukkeobi Sikdang, the signature dish is mugeunji deunggalbi-jjim: back ribs braised with mugeunji aged in earthenware jars. Mugeunji is sour, so it tastes different from the spicy seasoning.

At many shops, you fry rice in the leftover sauce after finishing the meat. Dain Maeun Deunggalbijjim also has cheese-topped fried rice on its menu.

If spicy food is hard for you, look at the menu on a map app before you decide.

3. Hanwoo BBQ - Korean beef grilled over charcoal

Hanwoo (한우) is Korea's native breed of cattle. Downtown Gyeongju has several restaurants where you grill hanwoo over charcoal.

Daereung Galbi is a butcher-shop restaurant. You choose and buy your meat at the butcher counter at the front, then sit down, pay a separate table-setting fee and grill it yourself. From the window seats on the 2nd floor, you can eat while watching the view at sunset.

Yeongyang Sutbul Galbi serves hanwoo galbisal (rib finger meat) and chimasal (flank) salt-grilled or marinated. The line is usually long, so it is best to arrive at opening time.

Hanwoo costs more than imported beef. That is because it is raised differently and for longer.

US cattle keep growing on pasture for a few more months after weaning, then eat grain in a feedlot for 4 to 6 months before going to market. In Australia, about 61% of the cattle slaughtered in the 3rd quarter of 2025 were raised only on pasture without going through a feedlot.

Hanwoo are raised in barns for about 30 months. From about 6 months they eat mixed grain feed together with roughage such as rice straw, and from about 13 months until going to market they are fattened on rich grain feed so that fat (marbling) forms between the lean meat.

In research by the National Institute of Animal Science, hanwoo marbling rose quickly after 16 months and was highest at 30 months. This is why hanwoo go to market at around 30 months. Feeding them that long is what makes the meat expensive.

Korea has been improving the hanwoo breed since 1969. According to the National Institute of Animal Science, the marbling score of steer hanwoo rose by 33% over the last 30 or so years. This means the taste and texture have become consistently better.

Imported beef comes a long way by ship, so it often arrives frozen. Hanwoo goes straight to market chilled within Korea, so the meat keeps its flavor.

Koreans love it for its taste, but it is expensive, so most people cannot eat it often. Google Maps reviews of Gyeongju hanwoo BBQ restaurants also often praise the quality of the meat.

4. Samgyeopsal and dwaeji-galbi - pork grilled over charcoal

Samgyeopsal (삼겹살) is pork belly. The Encyclopedia of Korean Culture describes it as the cut from the belly, after the ribs are removed, to the hind leg. The name means "three layers," because lean meat and fat form three layers.

Dwaeji-galbi (돼지갈비) is pork rib meat marinated in soy sauce seasoning and grilled. It is made the same way as bulgogi, so it is sweet and salty. The marinade burns easily, so at many shops the staff grill it for you.

The meat Koreans eat most

Koreans eat far more pork than beef or chicken. According to the Korea Rural Economic Institute, each person in Korea ate an estimated 29.2kg of pork in 2025. That is about the same as chicken (15.6kg) and beef (15.2kg) combined.

Among pork cuts, samgyeopsal is the most popular. When the same institute asked 507 consumers in 2024, 60.0% chose samgyeopsal as their favorite pork cut. Moksal (pork neck) came 2nd with 24.5%.

Why there are so many samgyeopsal restaurants

Samgyeopsal grilled on a hot plate the way it is today has a shorter history than you might think. The Encyclopedia of Korean Culture dates the first samgyeopsal grill restaurants to the late 1970s. A newspaper from 1983 already called Samgak-dong in Seoul a samgyeopsal alley.

In the 1980s, portable gas stoves spread to almost every home, so people could easily grill samgyeopsal not only at restaurants but also at home and outdoors. It was cheaper than beef, so it became a meat enjoyed by everyone from university students to office workers.

What visitors from abroad say

It is popular with foreigners too. In a 2026 survey of foreigners by the Korea Pork Board (한돈자조금관리위원회), samgyeopsal was the No. 1 favorite Korean food (57.2%).

It is eaten a lot abroad as well. In a 2025 survey by the Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs and the Korean Food Promotion Institute of 11,000 people in 22 countries, grilled samgyeopsal ranked 6th (4.5%) as the favorite Korean food.

Google Maps reviews of Seomin Sikdang in Gyeongju say the pork comes off the charcoal with a smoky aroma and a hint of sweetness. Reviews of a pork galbi restaurant in downtown Gyeongju also say its spicy, garlic-heavy marinade goes well with warm rice.

Samgyeopsal and dwaeji-galbi restaurants in Gyeongju

None of the four places below sells only samgyeopsal or dwaeji-galbi. The menus are varied, with marinated pork galbi, samgyeopsal, hanwoo rib meat, tteokgalbi (grilled short rib patties) and naengmyeon (cold noodles).

Even if each person in your group wants a different meat, you can choose at one restaurant. Look at the menu on a map app before you go.

At Seomin Sikdang, you do not have to grill the meat yourself. They grill marinated whole pork belly over charcoal, cut it and serve it. Some reviewers liked that their clothes did not smell of meat because they did not grill it themselves. There is no separate marinated pork galbi on the menu, but they also sell hanwoo charcoal tteokgalbi and spicy stir-fried webfoot octopus (jjukkumi).

Bomun Galbi is a pork galbi restaurant across from Gyeongju World (경주월드). The signature dish is its own house-marinated pork galbi. Google Maps reviews say the marinade uses bokbunja (Korean black raspberry), so savory flavor comes before sweetness, and that it is memorable because it tastes different from other galbi.

Bomun Galbi also has plain-grilled pork belly rib meat and Gyeongju hanwoo grade 1++ rib meat on the menu.

At Dongcheon Bugyeong Sutbul Galbi, you grill marinated pork galbi, marinated samgyeopsal and plain samgyeopsal yourself over charcoal. They also have hanwoo cow rib meat and salchisal (chuck flap), galbitang and naengmyeon.

At Dongcheon Bugyeong Sutbul Galbi, the first meat order is for 3 portions or more, and extra orders are for 2 portions or more.

Bonghwang Dwaejijip is downtown near Bonghwangdae (봉황대). It sells samgyeopsal, ogyeopsal (pork belly with skin), marinated galbi, LA galbi, fresh gabeurisal (a small cut from the top of the pork shoulder) and fresh pork ribs, so it is a good place to compare different cuts. Marinated galbi is ordered for 2 portions or more.

5. Bulgogi - thin marinated meat grilled over fire

Bulgogi (불고기) is thinly sliced meat marinated in soy sauce seasoning and grilled. A grilled meat dish called neobiani appears in records from the late 1800s, and it is said to have been called bulgogi from around the end of the Japanese colonial period.

Baengnyeon Sonnim (백년손님) The wire-grilled bulgogi is in the middle of the tray on the left, the grilled mackerel is in the center of the table and a stew is on the right.
Photo: Baengnyeon Sonnim (백년손님), 223-1 Hwangnam-dong, Gyeongju-si, Gyeongbuk (경북 경주시 황남동 223-1)

Bulgogi with broth and wire-grilled bulgogi

Bulgogi falls into two main types depending on how it is cooked. The Seoul style is cooked like a hot pot on a dome-shaped pan with broth and glass noodles. This is what many people picture when they think of bulgogi.

Seoksoe bulgogi (석쇠불고기) is marinated meat grilled crisp over charcoal on a seoksoe (wire grill). There is no broth, it has a strong smoky aroma, and the outside is lightly charred. Eonyang in Ulsan, close to Gyeongju, and Gwangyang in South Jeolla are famous for seoksoe bulgogi. Eonyang bulgogi is known for using only a little seasoning to bring out the flavor of the meat.

The seasoning is most often soy sauce or gochujang. Soy sauce seasoning is sweet and salty, and gochujang seasoning is spicy. Some shops let you choose between beef and pork. Some also sell ojingeo bulgogi, squid grilled in gochujang seasoning.

What visitors from abroad say

Bulgogi is one of the Korean dishes best known to foreigners. In a 2025 survey of Korean food consumers abroad, it ranked 4th as the favorite Korean food (5.6%) and 5th among Korean foods eaten most often in the past year (14.0%).

Google Maps reviews of Gyeongju's seoksoe bulgogi shops are full of praise too. One traveler from the Mediterranean region wrote that Korean food had not suited them but the bulgogi here was delicious, and a tour guide wrote that they stop by with their guests every time they visit Bulguksa (불국사).

Bulgogi restaurants in Gyeongju

Baengnyeon Sonnim is a place where people mostly eat soy sauce seoksoe bulgogi, gochujang seoksoe bulgogi and grilled mackerel. It is near Daereungwon and opens at 9 in the morning, so you can have breakfast there. It is closed on Wednesdays, and seoksoe bulgogi is ordered for 2 portions or more.

Gyeongju Dangol Sikdang is a place where you wrap squid bulgogi and spicy pork bulgogi in leaves. Since it appeared on TV it has become busy, and some reviewers waited almost an hour.

Chowon Seoksoe Bulgogi is on the restaurant street in front of Bulguksa. The seoksoe bulgogi set comes with doenjang-jjigae (soybean paste stew) and side dishes, so it is a good stop on the day you visit Bulguksa.
