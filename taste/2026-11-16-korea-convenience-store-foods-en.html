The Foods Foreigners Buy Most at Korean Convenience Stores, and What Koreans Buy Instead

For travelers in Korea, the convenience store is no longer just a quick stop. It is a destination. When the Korea Tourism Organization analyzed foreign card payments, visitors made about 13 million convenience store payments from January to July 2025, the most of any food category.

This guide picks the foods foreigners actually buy most, using store sales data and social media buzz. It explains why they are popular and how to eat them, and compares them with what Koreans pick at the same stores.

As of November 25, 2026 - Convenience store products change often, and not every store carries every item.

These picks are based on rankings that convenience store chains published after counting payments made with foreign cards, Alipay and WeChat Pay.

1. Why convenience stores

From January to September 2026, foreign sales at Korea's four big convenience store chains grew by around 70% from a year earlier. Much of it happens at night. Sales between 6 and 11 p.m. made up 46% of foreign sales at CU, 47% at GS25 and 41% at 7-Eleven.

Two reasons are usually given. First, it is the easiest place to taste the newest Korean food. Second, Korea feels safe at night. Social media is full of videos of foreigners walking down dark alleys to a convenience store for ramen and snacks.

On TikTok, videos tagged #koreanconveniencestore have more than 550 million views.

Busan is no different. From January to September 2026, foreign sales between 6 and 11 p.m. at GS25 stores in Busan rose 85% from a year earlier. In July 2026, foreign sales at CU stores grew most in Suyeong-gu, home of Gwangalli Beach, at 190%, followed by Haeundae-gu at 121%.

2. Banana milk, the first thing in the basket

Binggrae Banana Flavored Milk (바나나맛우유), sold in a yellow jar-shaped bottle, came out in 1974. It still sells about 1 million bottles a day on average and holds more than 80% of Korea's banana milk market.

It is also number one with foreigners. Over all of 2025, it was the product foreign shoppers bought most at both CU and GS25. In CU's 2025 analysis of foreign customers, banana milk was the top buy for both American and Chinese visitors, and number two for Japanese visitors.

The same is true in 2026. At 10 CU stores near the departure area of Incheon Airport, banana milk was the best seller from January to mid-August 2026. It is the last thing many people buy before leaving Korea. One GS25 store at Incheon Airport sold an average of 1,200 a day.

So convenience stores and Binggrae compete to win foreign shoppers with banana milk. Olive Young stores in Myeong-dong, Hongdae and Busan, as well as Incheon Airport, have shelves just for banana milk, and Binggrae keeps running promotions for foreign shoppers in tourist areas. In 2026, CU also handed out banana milk discount coupons to 60,000 foreign customers.

Why foreigners love it

The bottle - The round jar shape comes from the moon jar (달항아리, dalhangari), a white porcelain jar from the Joseon dynasty. The development team came up with it after seeing a moon jar at a ceramics fair. It looks great in photos, so many people snap a picture before the first sip.

Not too sweet - A review on the Thai travel forum Pantip said you cannot visit Korea without drinking banana milk, and that it is not too sweet, just right.

Dramas and social media - Travelers interviewed by SBS Biz said they go to convenience stores to taste the gimbap and banana milk they saw in dramas and on social media. A Korean Reddit user recommending convenience store food to foreign travelers also picked Yonsei Milk cream bread and banana milk.

The ttungba latte, a drink foreigners invented

The most famous way to drink it right now was created by foreign travelers at the convenience store counter. You pour banana milk over an ice cup and add a pouch of coffee. When Koreans picked it up, they called it a ttungba latte (뚱바라떼), from ttungba (뚱바), short for "chubby banana milk", a nickname for the round bottle.

Celebrities have tried it too. Model Hailey Bieber had a ttungba latte at a convenience store during her trip to Korea in May 2026 and posted on Instagram that she could not stop drinking it. You can see it in an Instagram post sharing Hailey Bieber's post.

Actress Emma Roberts posted an Instagram reel of herself making a drink with banana milk during her visit to Korea.

Singer Charlie Puth also posted a TikTok video of himself making a banana milk drink.

According to the US review site Yelp, searches for banana latte rose 1,573% in the year to June 2025. A combination that started in Korean convenience stores has spread to cafe menus abroad.

How to make a ttungba latte

1. Take an ice cup from the freezer. It is a plastic cup filled with ice.

2. Pour one banana milk into the ice cup.

3. Add a pouch of coffee from the drinks fridge. Hazelnut black coffee is the most popular. If the store has a coffee machine, you can use an espresso shot instead.

Good to know

The same company's strawberry milk and Melona-flavored milk also show up in foreign rankings. In 2025, strawberry milk ranked 4th in foreign sales at CU and 7th at GS25.

3. Greek yogurt, the new No. 1 that beat banana milk

Photos: GS25 (left), Market Kurly (right)

This is the product that climbed the most in 2026. In GS25's ranking of foreign sales from January to August 2026, YOZM Greek Yogurt Blueberry (요즘 그릭요거트) overtook banana milk to take first place.

Chinese-speaking travelers buy it the most. According to GS25, these shoppers buy a lot of dairy products such as milk and fermented milk, and this Greek yogurt is their top pick.

Why foreigners love it

Korean yogurt mukbang (eating videos) - Videos of thick yogurt topped with fruit or cereal went viral, mainly on Chinese social media. People visit convenience stores to try yogurt the Korean way.

Low sugar - Korea is in the middle of a low-sugar food boom. The low-sugar matcha Greek yogurt has just 2 g of sugar per cup, so you can eat it like a dessert without the guilt. At GS25, sales of low-sugar ice cream from April 1 to 17, 2025 were up 51% from a year earlier and made up 19% of all ice cream sales.

Earlier, in October 2025, GS25 launched YOZM Low-Sugar Matcha Greek Yogurt. It sold more than 3,000 cups on its first day and became the second-best-selling yogurt.

4. Gamdongran, the bathhouse egg in a convenience store

Gamdongran (감동란) is a soft-boiled egg sold in its shell. Even with the shell on, the white and yolk are already salted, so you do not need extra salt, and the yolk stays moist. The name means "an egg that moves you when you taste it".

In GS25's 2025 ranking of what foreign shoppers bought most, Gamdongran came second, right after banana milk. It was also in the top 5 foreign favorites at 7-Eleven.

The soft-boiled egg craze abroad

Korean-style soft-boiled eggs marinated in soy sauce are trending in the US. In early 2026, videos tagged Mayak Eggs (마약계란), showing a soft-boiled egg on rice with the yolk breaking open, spread on Reddit, Instagram and TikTok. One video passed 19 million views.

Gamdongran is the grab-and-go convenience store version of a seasoned soft-boiled egg. The US student food site Spoon University, writing about Korean convenience store food seen on TikTok, said the convenience store soft-boiled eggs looked delicious, especially in ramen.

Roasted eggs and sikhye at the jjimjilbang

When Koreans think of eggs as a snack, the first place that comes to mind is the jjimjilbang (찜질방), a Korean bathhouse and sauna. There, people eat brown roasted eggs (맥반석 계란, maekbanseok gyeran), slowly cooked for hours in a hot room, with sikhye (식혜), a sweet rice drink. Along with towels folded into "sheep head" hats, they are the symbol of the jjimjilbang.

There is a reason they go together. A roasted egg has a chewy white and a dry, crumbly yolk that sticks in your throat. A cold sikhye washes it down.

Convenience stores also sell roasted eggs and sikhye in cans or bottles, so you can try the pairing without going to a jjimjilbang. Gamdongran is the same kind of egg snack, but its yolk is moist, so it is easy to eat even without a drink.

Why foreigners love it

No salt needed - It is already seasoned, so you can peel it and eat it on the street.

Hard to copy at home - One Reddit user said the boiled eggs come in three or four flavors, all cooked just right, and taste much better than the ones they make at home.

With ramen - Adding Gamdongran to spicy cup ramen is a classic Korean convenience store combo.

5. Triangle gimbap and gimbap, Korea's lunch

Triangle gimbap (삼각김밥, samgak gimbap) is rice pressed into a triangle and wrapped in seaweed (gim). Fillings include tuna mayo, bulgogi and kimchi. The seaweed is packed separately, so it stays crisp until you peel off the film right before eating.

Gimbap and triangle gimbap are the meals Koreans buy most often at convenience stores. Near lunchtime, the gimbap shelves at stores in office districts empty fast. These days Koreans also think about health. Two high-protein gimbap that GS25 launched in late April 2026 sold 1.5 million in three months, and sales of gimbap and triangle gimbap each rose 25% over the same period.

Foreigners buy a lot of gimbap too. In CU's 2025 analysis of foreign customers, bulgogi gimbap was the third most bought product among American visitors.

After the Netflix film KPop Demon Hunters became a worldwide hit, more foreigners went looking for the gimbap and ramen they saw in it, and the number of payments with overseas cards at CU rose 185% in July and August 2025.

What travelers say

A convenience store worker interviewed by Vietnamese outlet Zing News said banana milk, triangle gimbap and sandwiches sell out as soon as they are put on the shelf.

One Reddit user said triangle gimbap is filling and comes in many flavors, and told first-timers to learn how to open the wrapper first.

How to open triangle gimbap

1. Pull the strip marked 1 at the top center all the way down.

2. Pull the left side of the film (marked 2) off to the side.

3. Pull the right side of the film (marked 3) off to the side, and the seaweed wraps around the rice.

Warm up your gimbap

After you pay, heat gimbap in the store's microwave for about 30 seconds. The rice gets softer.

6. Ramen, from souvenir to an experience

Ramen keeps showing up on foreign best-seller lists. Sesame Ramen (참깨라면) was in GS25's top 10 foreign food sales from January to August 2025, and GS25's 2026 list of foreign favorites included Sesame Ramen and Buldak (불닭볶음면, spicy chicken stir-fried noodles). In CU's survey of foreign purchases in fall 2025, Jin Ramen Spicy (진라면 매운맛) came fourth.

Cooking ramen right in the store has become an experience in itself. Almost every convenience store has a hot water dispenser for cup ramen, and some have instant ramen machines that cook packet ramen for you.

In Busan, several convenience stores let you eat ramen from an instant ramen machine while looking at the sea. Locations and how to use the machine are in Where to Eat Ramyeon (Korean Ramen) by the Sea in Busan.

7. Roasted sweet potatoes and desserts

Roasted sweet potatoes

Roasted sweet potatoes, baked in a machine next to the counter, are catching on fast with foreigners too. CU's foreign sales of roasted sweet potatoes from January to September 2026 were up 127% from a year earlier. They are popular with Koreans as well. At CU in 2025, roasted sweet potatoes outsold hoppang (호빵), the classic steamed winter bun, by about three times.

CU uses the Beniharuka variety, known in Korea as honey sweet potato (꿀고구마, kkul goguma).

Dubai chewy cookie

This is a chewy Korean take on Dubai chocolate, nicknamed dujjonku (두쫀쿠) in Korea. It ranked third in GS25 sales to Chinese-speaking shoppers from January to August 2026. CU's Dubai dessert products passed 10 million in total sales in early February 2026.

Trendy desserts like this arrive fast in Korean convenience stores and change just as fast. The LA Times reported that up to 70 new food items appear in Korean convenience stores every week. On your next trip, a different dessert may be number one.

Honey butter almonds

A popular souvenir. They also made GS25's 2026 list of foreign favorites.

8. Mix it like a Korean: convenience store combos

In Korean convenience stores, many people mix several products into a new dish instead of eating each one as it is. The convenience store chains make these combos themselves and post them on their official social media.

9. So what do Koreans buy?

Foreigners account for 2.5% of CU's total sales (3rd quarter of 2026). That means most of the best-seller lists convenience stores publish reflect what Koreans choose. Look at those lists, and what foreigners and Koreans want from a convenience store turns out to be quite different.

Ulbaksa, a bathhouse drink in a can

Photo: Dong-A Pharmaceutical (left)

GS25's top hit product of 2025 was Ulbaksa (얼박사). The name comes from the first syllables of ice cup (얼음컵, eoreum-keop), Bacchus (박카스, an energy tonic) and cider (사이다, lemon-lime soda). It puts in one can the mix Koreans used to make at jjimjilbangs and PC bangs (internet cafes): Bacchus with cider over ice.

When GS25 looked at its receipt data, the top item bought together with Bacchus was an ice cup, and the second was cider. So it turned the mix into a real product with Dong-A Pharmaceutical, the maker of Bacchus. It sold more than 2.5 million cans in two months and became GS25's best-selling drink.

Launched in June 2025, Ulbaksa sold 35 million cans in its first year. A sugar-free Ulbaksa Zero came out in March 2026 and passed 6 million cans in three months.

Men make up 73% of buyers, and it is popular with university students in their early 20s and office workers in their 30s and 40s. For Koreans, it is a drink for when you are tired.

Protein drinks and protein bars: Koreans building their bodies

While foreigners reach for banana milk, Koreans reach for protein. GS25's protein drink sales in the first half of 2026 rose 27% from a year earlier and passed banana milk sales by 4% for the first time. GS25 went from 3 kinds of protein drinks in 2020 to about 50, and sales grew 15-fold.

Behind this is a fitness boom. In the 2020s, the body profile (바디프로필, bapeu (바프) for short) became a trend among Koreans in their 20s and 30s. People spend months shaping their bodies through exercise and diet, then take photos at a studio and post them on social media. As more people went to the gym to get in shape, the habit of eating protein to build muscle spread too.

Diets changed as well. Cutting carbs and eating more protein became popular, and in 2026 the "switch-on diet", which replaces two meals a day with protein shakes, caught on among women in their 20s and 30s. Protein drinks are no longer just a post-workout supplement. They have become breakfast or lunch.

It is not just drinks. Convenience stores now carry protein bars, and even breads and bagels with added protein. GS25 went from about 20 kinds of protein bars in 2025 to about 30 in 2026, and 7-Eleven's protein bar sales from January to October 2026 rose 44% from a year earlier. Most buyers are in their 20s and 30s. This age group makes up 62% of protein drink and bar sales at CU, and 70% of protein snack purchases at GS25.

Photos: GS25

In Korea, enjoying tasty food while looking after your health is called healthy pleasure. That is why so many protein drinks now come in familiar flavors like chocolate, banana and strawberry. The high-protein gimbap from section 5 is part of the same trend.

Desserts and alcohol

Besides Ulbaksa, GS25's top 7 hit products of 2025 included alcoholic drinks such as the Ahn Sung-jae Sauvignon Lemon Blanc Highball and Sunyang Oak Soju, and desserts such as the Seoul Milk Milk Cream Mochi Roll and the Ice Brulee. The Seoul Milk dessert series has sold more than 6 million in total and took all of the top 10 spots among GS25's roughly 200 chilled dessert breads.

Foreigners and Koreans, the difference

Foreigners buy what you can only taste in Korea: classic Korean flavors like banana milk, Gamdongran and gimbap, or combos they saw on social media.

Koreans buy energy and health: Ulbaksa for when they are tired, protein drinks and protein-boosted gimbap.

There is one thing both share: pouring something over an ice cup. Foreigners make a ttungba latte, Koreans make Ulbaksa.
