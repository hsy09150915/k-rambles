Seoul to Gyeongju: KTX vs Express Bus, Plus What to Eat at the Rest Stop

From Seoul to Gyeongju takes a little over 2 hours by KTX and about 3 hours 30 minutes by express bus. If you want to get there fast, take the KTX. If you'd rather arrive right in the middle of the city and see a Korean highway rest stop on the way, take the express bus. This guide compares the two and covers what to enjoy at the rest stop the bus pulls into.

As of November 17, 2026 — number of departures and times can change. Check again when you book.

The KTX is fast and frequent, but it stops at Gyeongju Station (KTX), away from the city center, so you have to take one more city bus.

The express bus takes longer, but it drops you at the terminal in the city, a 10-minute walk from Hwangnidan-gil. It also stops at a highway rest stop on the way.

1. By KTX

The KTX is Korea's high-speed train. In Seoul, trains to Gyeongju leave from three stations: Seoul Station, Suseo Station and Cheongnyangni Station. All of them arrive at Gyeongju Station (KTX).

From Seoul Station to Gyeongju Station takes a little over 2 hours, with more than 20 trains a day. From Suseo Station it is also a little over 2 hours.

From Cheongnyangni Station, the KTX-Eum runs to Gyeongju Station. It follows the Jungang Line south and takes about 2 hours 50 minutes, with fewer trains than from Seoul Station.

Trains from Suseo Station used to be SRT, run by a different company, so you had to book them separately. Since KTX and SRT merged in September 2026, you can book Seoul Station and Suseo Station trains in the same place. How to book is covered in How to book the KTX.

Since October 2026, you can book from two months before departure. Weekend and holiday trains fill up fast, so book as soon as your plans are set.

Gyeongju Station (KTX) is away from the city center. The city buses from the station to Hwangnidan-gil are covered in From Gyeongju Station into the city.

Seoul Express Bus Terminal has separate buildings for the Gyeongbu Line and the Honam Line. Buses to Gyeongju leave from the Gyeongbu Line side.

You can buy tickets at the terminal ticket office or a self-service ticket machine. Three bus companies take turns running the route, so just check the departure time and boarding gate number on your ticket.

Express buses come in different classes. The class is shown on the timetable, so check it when you buy your ticket.

ClassSeats
Standard (일반)4 seats per row. The least legroom.
Deluxe (우등)3 seats per row, 28 seats in all. Wider seats that recline a lot.
Premium (프리미엄)21 seats, fewer than deluxe. The seats recline almost flat, and each has a privacy screen and its own video screen.

The ride is over 3 hours, so if the times are the same, deluxe or premium is far more comfortable.

3. Pros and cons of the express bus

ProsCons
It drops you right at the terminal in the middle of Gyeongju. No need to change to a city bus with your luggage.It takes over an hour longer than the KTX.
It usually costs less than the KTX.On weekends and holidays, traffic can make you arrive late.
It stops at a highway rest stop on the way, so you can try Korean rest stop food.With about 10 a day, it runs less often than the KTX.

Rest stops count as one of the small pleasures of traveling in Korea. Even the Korean government's English promotional site notes that the train is punctual, but you miss out on highway rest stop food.

4. The rest stop on the way

Express buses from Seoul to Gyeongju don't take the Gyeongbu Expressway; they take the Jungbu Naeryuk Expressway and the Sangju–Yeongcheon Expressway. So they don't stop at Geumgang Rest Area on the Gyeongbu Expressway.

Many buses stop at Nakdonggang Rest Area on the Sangju–Yeongcheon Expressway. Heading to Gyeongju it's called Nakdonggang Uiseong Rest Area, and heading back to Seoul Nakdonggang Gumi Rest Area. These are the names for each direction of the same rest area.

Depending on the bus, it may stop at another rest area instead, such as Mungyeong Saejae Rest Area on the Jungbu Naeryuk Expressway. Which one depends on the bus company and departure time, and the driver will tell you when you arrive.

Here's what's worth doing there.

  • Hodu-gwaja (walnut cakes): Small walnut-shaped cakes filled with sweet red bean paste and a piece of walnut. One of the best-selling snacks at Korean rest stops.
  • Sotteok-sotteok (소떡소떡): A skewer of sausages and rice cakes, alternated and grilled. It became the signature rest stop snack after appearing on TV in 2018.
  • A meal at the food court: Quick meals like budae-jjigae, bibimbap, donkatsu and udon. Budae-jjigae is a stew with ham, sausage and ramen noodles.
  • Restroom: It's more than another hour from the rest stop to Gyeongju, so use the restroom here.

Many food courts take orders at a kiosk. You can choose English, Chinese or Japanese at the top of the screen, so you can order without knowing Korean.

Remember this at the rest stop

Nakdonggang Rest Area in particular is a transfer stop where express buses to many different places stop together. Lots of similar-looking buses are parked there.

Before you get off, take a photo of your bus company's name and the bus's license plate number, and be sure to note the departure time the driver gives.

5. Straight from Incheon Airport to Gyeongju

If you're heading to Gyeongju right after landing at Incheon Airport, there's a bus that goes all the way without passing through Seoul.

ServiceAbout 10 a day; the first leaves between 7 and 8 a.m. and the last between 11 p.m. and midnight
Travel timeA little over 4 hours

Going from the airport to Seoul Station and taking the KTX doesn't make a big difference in total time to Gyeongju. With a lot of luggage, the bus with no transfers is much easier.

This is an intercity bus, so you can buy tickets at the airport bus ticket office, or in advance on the intercity bus English booking site introduced in Busan to Gyeongju by train or bus.

If your flight lands late

Immigration and baggage claim can take over an hour. Pick a bus that leaves well after your flight's arrival time.

6. Can you do Gyeongju as a day trip from Seoul?

With the KTX, a day trip is possible. But Gyeongju's sights are spread across the city center, Bulguksa and the Bomun area, so seeing everything in one day is hard.

LegTravel time
Seoul Station to Gyeongju Station (KTX)A little over 2 hours
Gyeongju Station (KTX) to the city centerAbout 20 minutes by city bus
Return tripA little over 2 hours 30 minutes
Total travel timeA little over 5 hours

The last KTX from Gyeongju Station (KTX) to Seoul Station leaves around 10:30 p.m. If you take a KTX around 7 a.m., you'll have about 10 hours in Gyeongju.

That's enough for Bulguksa and the city center. See Bulguksa first in the morning, walk the city center in the afternoon, then catch Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond lit up at dusk before heading back.

Suggested order for a day trip

Gyeongju Station (KTX)BulguksaHwangnidan-gilDaereungwonCheomseongdaeDonggung and WoljiGyeongju Station (KTX)
  • Gyeongju Station (KTX) to Bulguksa: A little over an hour on bus 700. It passes Hwangnidan-gil on the way to Bulguksa.
  • Bulguksa to the city center: 30 to 40 minutes on bus 700, 10 or 11.
  • Walking the city center: Walk from Hwangnidan-gil to Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond. The route is in Gyeongju downtown walking route.
  • Getting back: Allow a little over 30 minutes by city bus from Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond to Gyeongju Station (KTX). If you're catching the last train, a taxi is less stressful.
Better to skip on a day trip

Seokguram means changing at Bulguksa to bus 12, which runs once an hour, so adding it to a one-day plan cuts into your time in the city center.

The Bomun area is a lakeside cluster of resorts and parks, best for staying and relaxing. On a day trip, it's fine to skip.

If you want to see all three, go for 2 days and 1 night. Spend one day on the city center and Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond at night, and the next on Bulguksa, Seokguram and the Bomun area, and you can take your time.

8. Check before you go

  • Departure station: KTX trains leave separately from Seoul Station, Suseo Station and Cheongnyangni Station. Check the departure station on your ticket.
  • Day trip: Book your return KTX in advance and check the last train time.
  • Gyeongju Station (KTX): It's away from the city center, so add about 20 minutes by city bus.
  • Express bus boarding: At Seoul Express Bus Terminal, board on the Gyeongbu Line side.
  • Rest stop: Remember your bus's license plate number and departure time.
