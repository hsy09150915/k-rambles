How to Ride the Busan Subway Without Getting Confused

Direction, transfers, exits — learn to read the signs ahead of time and you'll rarely get caught off guard on the subway.

Last checked September 11, 2026 — station signage design can change with renovations.

The full Busan Metro map — each line has its own color.

Foreign travelers tend to run into four main difficulties on the Busan subway.

First, figuring out which direction to go.

Second, not knowing where to transfer.

Third, not being able to find the right exit.

Fourth, not knowing where the restrooms or elevators are.

This guide covers all four.

First, the one app you'll need

The Kakao Subway app comes up throughout this guide, so it's worth installing ahead of time. Installation and language settings are covered in our map & subway app setup guide.

Before the Gate: Understand Platform Layouts

Korean subway stations come in two platform layouts.

In an island platform, the platform sits in the middle and trains stop on both sides. Watch the direction carefully before boarding.

The other is a side platform, where the tracks run down the middle and the platforms face opposite directions. Check carefully when you tap in at the gate.

Knowing which layout you're dealing with means no surprises once you're down on the platform.

Lines Are Color-Coded

Every Korean subway line has its own color. In Busan, Line 1 is orange and Line 2 is green.

Switch Kakao Subway's settings to English and the Busan Metro map shows both Korean and English spellings together.

Keep that in mind when searching on a map app, so not knowing the spelling doesn't leave you stuck.

Before You Leave: Check These in Kakao Subway

To figure out subway direction, check the following in Kakao Subway — both the English spelling and the Korean shape:

  • The next station after where you're boarding
  • The last station on that line
  • The transfer station's name

Remember "OO행" and "OO방면"

You'll see the Hangul "OO행" or "OO방면" on station signage ("-haeng" means "bound for"; "-bangmyeon" means "toward").

That's not the next station — it's attached after the name of that line's very last station.

It marks the train's direction of travel. When you see these characters, just match them against the last-station name you already checked in Kakao Subway.

One More Check Before You Board

The display above the doors shows the current station, the next station, and the line color.

Double-check the direction before you get on.

Finding Your Exit

Exit numbers are marked right after you pass through the gate.

Busan uses yellow-and-green "Way Out" signs (Seoul uses "Exit").

Restrooms and Elevators

Restroom signs are posted alongside the exit signs too.

If you're hauling a heavy suitcase, look for the elevator instead of the stairs.

Making a Transfer

Transferring is fairly easy if you follow the line colors. When you get off at a transfer station, there's usually a colored band directly ahead marking the line you're switching to.

Transfer Discounts

To get the transfer discount, you need to tap back through the gate within 30 minutes of getting off.

See the table below for which combinations connect for free.
