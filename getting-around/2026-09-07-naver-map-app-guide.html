Google Maps Will Get You Lost in Busan — Use a Korean Map App Instead

Three apps worth installing before a Busan trip — get English station names and directions sorted with Kakao Subway, then set up search and arrival alerts in Naver Map and Kakao Map.

Last checked September 10, 2026 — the apps' screens can change with updates.

The three apps covered in this guide — Naver Map, Kakao Map, and Kakao Subway.

Why these apps

Getting transit info right in Busan is easier with a few apps installed ahead of time.

Most tourists default to Google Maps, but since it's not built by a local company, it tends to be less accurate here — plenty of reviews back that up. Try a Korea-specific app instead — it's noticeably more reliable.

So here are three apps worth installing before a Korea (Busan) trip:

  • Kakao Subway
  • Naver Map
  • Kakao Map

Android and iOS screens may differ slightly, but the steps are the same (the screenshots below are from Android).

1. Kakao Subway

There are two reasons to use Kakao Subway. Korean place names can look unfamiliar in English, so knowing a station's official English spelling ahead of time helps both when riding the subway and when searching for it in a map app. It also shows the route, arrival time, and first/last train times all in one place.

  1. 1

    Install it, then open the settings menu

    Install "Kakao Subway" from the app store. Only English and Korean are supported. Open the menu in the top left and go to Settings.

  2. 2

    Switch the language to English

    In Language Settings, set both Transit Map Language and App Language to English.

  3. 3

    Select the city you're visiting

    Choose Busan under Select City.

  4. 4

    Set your departure and arrival stations

    Tapping a station brings up From, Via, To, and Info all at once. Set your departure station as From and your destination as To.

    English search doesn't work

    The search bar at the top only works in Korean. Since English search isn't supported, try tapping the station directly on the route map to set From/To.

  5. 5

    Check the route results

    You'll see travel time, fare, and transfer station info. Tap the map icon in the bottom right for a simple visual of the route.

Figuring out subway direction

Figuring out which direction to go is usually the most confusing part of riding the subway. Each line in Korea has its own color, and locals often navigate by color too. To work out the direction, check the following:

  • The name of the last station in your direction of travel on that line (English and Korean)
  • The name of the next station in your direction from where you're boarding (English and Korean)
  • The end-station name of the line you're transferring to
  • The name of the next station in your direction from the transfer station

Sometimes a major midway station is marked too, but knowing the above is usually enough to find your direction. Korean subway stations mark direction and transfer routes everywhere — on wall pillars, above the ticket gates, above the platform doors. (We'll cover this in a separate piece.)

Checking first/last train and arrival times

Take Seomyeon, a transfer station, as an example. Tap the station and info pops up — since it's a transfer station, you pick a line first. Tap Schedule to see the timetable.

The schedule only shows in Korean. It's split into 평일 (weekday), 토요일 (Saturday), and 공휴일 (holiday) — Busan Transportation Corporation doesn't list Sunday separately, and Kakao Subway appears to follow the same convention.

Checking it directly, Line 1 and Line 2's first/last train times were identical on Saturdays and holidays, and it's safe to assume Sunday runs on the same schedule too. The total number of trains per day can be slightly lower on holidays than Saturdays, but that's not something a traveler really needs to worry about.

2. Naver Map

Install "Naver Map" from the app store.

  1. 1

    Set the language

    Change the language to English in settings.

  2. 2

    Start by searching a region or neighborhood name

    Try typing the region name in Romanized letters in the search bar. If you're trying to search for a subway station, use the English spelling shown in Kakao Subway — that cuts down on the trouble of not knowing the tricky Korean spelling.

  3. 3

    Search a specific category keyword

    With the neighborhood you want already showing on screen, search a category like "cafe" and nearby spots show up with their English names. Keep in mind that if you search a keyword without first searching a destination, it searches based on whatever location the map is currently showing.

  4. 4

    Set your destination and check transit options

    Search your destination, tap the pin, then set it as "To" — the available transit options will show up. Double-check that your starting point and destination are correct.

  5. 5

    "Go" for an arrival alert

    Pick one of the transit options and tap "Go" — it'll alert you before you reach your destination. To turn the alert off, tap "End directions" if it's showing; if not, it's in the menu at the bottom right.

    Don't rely on the alert alone

    Since the subway runs underground, location tracking can be unreliable, and you might miss your stop. Treat the alert as a backup — it's safer to keep checking for yourself.

3. Kakao Map

  1. 1

    Set the language

    Tap the More (···) menu in the bottom right, then change the language from the gear (settings) icon. There's no English label reading "Language" — it's only written in Korean as "언어."

  2. 2

    Search your destination and tap the transit icon

    Set your destination as To and check that your starting point is right. One thing to know — the driving route shows first at the top, so tap the second, bus-shaped icon to see transit options. Unlike Kakao Subway, Kakao Map does support English search.

  3. 3

    Start navigation for an arrival alert

    Choose a transit route, tap "Start Navigation" on the option you want, and just like Naver Map, it'll sound an alert once you're near your destination.

Good to know

  • Some places still show up Romanized or mixed with Korean — still a lot more usable than Korean-only.
  • The language setting, search, and directions all work fine without logging in.
  • Every "View on map" link on this site opens the exact right spot regardless of the app's language, as long as the app is installed.
