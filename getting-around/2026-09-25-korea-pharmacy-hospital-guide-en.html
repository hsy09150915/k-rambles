Sick in Korea? Convenience Store, Pharmacy, or Hospital

From convenience-store meds to hospital departments to the 3rd-tier hospital rule, in the order you'd actually need them.

As of September 2026

Getting sick while traveling is stressful enough without a language barrier on top. This guide walks through it in order — convenience store → pharmacy → hospital — so you know exactly what you can do at each stage.

1. What you can buy at a convenience store

Korean law limits which over-the-counter meds convenience stores can sell. As of September 2026, there are 11 approved items:

  • Pain/fever relievers — 3 types, including Tylenol (타이레놀)
  • Cold medicine — 2 types, including Panpyrin (판피린)
  • Digestive aids — 4 types, including Beajejeong (베아제정)
  • Pain-relief patches — 2 types, including Jeil Cool Pap (제일쿨파프)
The list is set to grow in December 2026

Korea's Ministry of Health and Welfare has announced plans to expand the convenience-store list to up to 20 items (digestive medicine, anti-diarrheal medicine, and artificial tears are among the items being discussed). This hasn't taken effect yet, so if you're visiting after December, you may see more options than the list above.

2. Finding a pharmacy

If convenience-store meds aren't enough, you'll need an actual pharmacy. Searching "pharmacy" in English on Naver Map doesn't turn up much — search the Korean word "약국" instead, or use "pharmacy" on Google Maps, which works better in English. Pharmacies usually have a green cross sign, but plenty don't display one, so searching a map is far more reliable than looking for the sign.

Here are a few late-hour pharmacies near the main tourist areas.

Haeundae 365 Hana Pharmacy (LCT building, 2F) — daily 9:00 AM–12:01 AM (past midnight) Marine Pharmacy · Miyu Pharmacy — daily, roughly 9:00 AM–11:00 PM
Nampo · Jagalchi Gukje Pharmacy — daily 8:30 AM–11:00 PM Busan Ready Young Pharmacy · Better:me Pharmacy — daily 9:00 AM–11:00 PM
Seomyeon Seomyeon 365 Pharmacy · Seomyeon Jjang Pharmacy — daily 9:00 AM–11:00 PM
Gwangalli Sejong Pharmacy — Tue–Sun 12:01 PM–1:00 AM the next day (closed Mondays), the latest we found anywhere in Busan Blue Lagoon Pharmacy — daily 10:00 AM–11:00 PM

Double-check the exact hours on a map app right before you go — pharmacy hours change fairly often.

4. How Korea's hospital tiers work — you can't just walk into a "3rd-tier" hospital

Korean hospitals are grouped into 1st, 2nd, and 3rd tiers by size. In terms of staff, facilities, and equipment, 3rd-tier ("advanced general") hospitals really are the biggest and most specialized — that part is true. The catch isn't quality, it's access: you generally can't go without a referral letter.

1st tier — neighborhood clinics (의원) Under 30 beds. For everyday stuff like colds or indigestion. Walk in, no appointment needed.
2nd tier — hospitals / general hospitals (병원) 30+ beds, multiple departments. Also walk-in — no referral required.
3rd tier — advanced general hospitals (상급종합병원) University-hospital level, only 47 nationwide (Ministry of Health and Welfare's 5th-cycle designation, 2024–2026). In principle you need a referral letter from a 1st- or 2nd-tier facility — without one, you can be turned away unless it's a genuine emergency.
1st- and 2nd-tier clinics close for holidays and cut Saturday hours short

Most 1st-tier clinics and 2nd-tier hospitals are closed entirely on public holidays, and on Saturdays they usually don't shut down — they just close early, often around 1:00 PM. Unless it's a large hospital with an emergency room, don't assume it's open at any hour. Check a map app (like Naver Map) for that day's actual hours before you head over.

Unless it's a serious accident or condition, always start at a 1st-tier clinic. Busan only has these 4 advanced general (3rd-tier) hospitals — if a place isn't on this list, whatever "hospital" you find on a map is almost certainly 1st- or 2nd-tier.

  • Pusan National University Hospital (Seo-gu)
  • Dong-A University Hospital (Seo-gu)
  • Inje University Busan Paik Hospital (Busanjin-gu)
  • Kosin University Gospel Hospital (Seo-gu)
