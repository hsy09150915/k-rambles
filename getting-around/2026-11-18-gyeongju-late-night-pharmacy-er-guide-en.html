Sick at Night in Gyeongju: Late-Night Pharmacies and Emergency Rooms

Where to go in Gyeongju when you need medicine late at night or have to see a doctor.

For medicines you can buy at convenience stores, Korean names of hospital departments and how Korean hospitals are organized, see Sick in Korea? Convenience Store, Pharmacy, or Hospital.

As of November 18, 2026 — pharmacy and hospital hours can change. Call once more before you go.

1. Six public late-night pharmacies open until 11 p.m.

A public late-night pharmacy (공공심야약국) is a pharmacy that Gyeongju City has designated to stay open late. It works like any other pharmacy during the day and, on set days, stays open from 8 p.m. to 11 p.m.

The Gyeongju Public Health Center lists six of them. Each pharmacy stays open late on different days.

The Gyeongju Public Health Center says hours may change and asks visitors to call ahead. Tap the pharmacy name on the map to see its phone number.

None near Bomun Lake Resort or Bulguksa Temple

All six are in downtown Gyeongju and the area to its north (Seongdong-dong, Hwangseong-dong, Dongcheon-dong, Yonggang-dong). If you are staying near Bomun Lake Resort or Bulguksa Temple, you will need to take a taxi into town.

The surest option is to buy any medicine you might suddenly need at night during the day.

2. On Sundays and public holidays

No public late-night pharmacy is open on Sunday nights. On public holidays, Hansol Pharmacy and Gyeongju Plaza Pharmacy are closed, and the others may not open either.

On Sundays and public holidays, you need to look up which pharmacies are open that day.

  • Emergency Medical Portal E-Gen: Its pharmacy search and hospital search show places open right now on a map. The site is in Korean only, so ask a Korean nearby, such as your hotel staff, to open it and look for you.
  • 119: Even if it is not an emergency, 119 can tell you which pharmacies and hospitals are open now.
  • 1330 Korea Travel Hotline: A phone line run by the Korea Tourism Organization. It can interpret for you if you cannot communicate at a pharmacy or hospital.

If every pharmacy is closed, convenience stores sell fever and pain relievers, cold medicine, digestive aids and pain relief patches. See the main guide for the full list.

3. 24-hour emergency rooms

Gyeongju has two hospitals with a 24-hour emergency room. You can go to an emergency room directly, without a referral.

Dongguk University Gyeongju Hospital also has emergency care for infants and young children. Pediatric specialists see patients from 5:30 p.m. on weekdays, from 12:30 p.m. on Saturdays, and from 9 a.m. on Sundays and public holidays, until 9 a.m. the next day.

For mild symptoms, see a local clinic during the day instead

Emergency rooms treat the most urgent patients first, so with mild symptoms you may wait a long time. It also costs much more than a daytime visit to a local clinic.

If it is urgent, for example trouble breathing, confusion or heavy bleeding, call 119 right away. 119 can connect you to an interpreter.

4. Large hospitals (tertiary hospitals)

Gyeongju has no tertiary hospital (상급종합병원, the top level of Korean hospitals). The nearest are Ulsan University Hospital in Ulsan and five hospitals in Daegu (Kyungpook National University Hospital, Keimyung University Dongsan Hospital, Daegu Catholic University Hospital, Yeungnam University Hospital and Kyungpook National University Chilgok Hospital).

If you get sick while traveling in Gyeongju, start with one of the emergency rooms above or a local clinic. If you need a bigger hospital, the doctor will write a referral, and in an emergency 119 will take you to the right hospital.

Tertiary hospitals are designated again every three years, and the new list that takes effect in 2027 is expected at the end of 2026. We will check this list again once it is announced.
