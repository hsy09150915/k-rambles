How to Shop and Get Your Tax Refund in Busan

Comparing 4 duty-free options, how immediate refunds work at Olive Young and E-Mart, and the Gimhae Airport hours you can’t miss.

As of September 2026 — the duty-free/refund industry changes often, so it’s worth double-checking before you go.

Most shopping-and-tax headaches in Busan come down to two things. One is not knowing the refund process well enough to actually get the money you’re owed. The other is misjudging the timing and missing the refund at the airport. Both are real money, so let’s go through this in order.

Duty-Free — At the Airport, or Downtown Ahead of Time

Korea runs both airport duty-free and downtown duty-free shops. That second concept doesn’t exist in the US or Europe at all — it’s an East Asian pattern shared by Korea, Japan, Hong Kong, Thailand, and Taiwan, so Western travelers may not have heard of it before.

Busan has 2 downtown duty-free shops, 1 airport duty-free, and 1 port duty-free — 4 in total. Duty-free means the price already has no tax added, so all you need is your passport and flight info — there’s no separate refund process afterward.

The “Shinsegae Duty Free” that used to be at Centum City was a separately licensed duty-free operation, and it closed on January 24, 2025 due to financial trouble. “Shinsegae Department Store” is a completely different business and is still open as normal — as a regular department store, it still offers the tax refund service covered in the next section.

Tax Refund — Pay Tax Now, Get It Back Later

This is the system for regular stores like Olive Young, Daiso, and department stores — you pay the tax-included price, then get that VAT back later. The basic rules:

  • You need to spend at least ₩15,000 per receipt
  • Take your purchases out of Korea within 3 months of the purchase date
  • If there’s no “Tax Free” logo sticker near the entrance or register, that store doesn’t offer refunds at all — check before you buy
No refund on fish at Jagalchi — because there’s no tax on it to begin with

Unprocessed agricultural, livestock, and seafood products (live fish, dried seafood, vegetables, fruit, etc.) are legally exempt from VAT in Korea (Article 26 of Korea’s Value-Added Tax Act). So when you buy live fish or seafood as-is at a market like Jagalchi, there’s no tax on it to refund — the price is already tax-free.

On the other hand, anything seasoned or cooked counts as a “processed product” and does carry tax — packaged seasoned seafood, flavored dried fish, or a cooked raw-fish set meal at a restaurant, for example, don’t qualify for this exemption.

Most of the Time, It’s Done at the Register

Most “Tax Free” stores support immediate refunds — if you spend under ₩1,000,000 at one store, just show your passport at checkout and you’ll pay the tax-free price on the spot. No airport trip, no paperwork.

That said, the exact process varies a bit by store:

  • Olive Young: Show your passport at checkout and the discount is applied instantly
  • E-Mart: You pay full price first, then before you leave, show your receipt and passport at the customer service desk — they’ll refund the difference straight to your card (or cash, if you paid cash)

What If You Spend Over ₩1,000,000?

If you spend more than ₩1,000,000 at one store, only the amount over that limit skips the immediate refund and gets handled with paperwork (a refund voucher) instead.

  • At checkout, you get a refund voucher for the excess amount — it has the processing company’s logo printed on it (e.g. the green Global Tax Free logo)
  • That same day, at any other store carrying the same logo (it doesn’t have to be that specific store), you can put a card down as collateral and get cash on the spot
  • That said, on the day you leave, customs at Gimhae Airport still needs to confirm you’re actually taking the item out of the country — skip that, and the amount gets charged to whatever card you put down
  • If you’d rather skip the hassle, you can just handle everything at the airport on your departure day — getting it downtown early is really just an option for when you want the cash in hand sooner
Duty-free shopping is a separate thing

Everything above applies only to items you actually paid tax on. Anything bought at a duty-free shop was never taxed in the first place, so this whole refund process doesn’t apply to it.

Gimhae Airport — Not a 24-Hour Counter

Careful if your flight is after 9pm or early morning

Incheon Airport has both a staffed counter and unmanned kiosks, both open 24 hours. Gimhae Airport only has a staffed counter, open 6:30am–9pm. It’s at Gate 4 on the 2nd floor.

If your flight falls outside those hours, you may not be able to get your refund at the airport at all. In that case, it’s safer to get it in advance at Shinsegae Centum City (above) or one of the unmanned kiosks below.
