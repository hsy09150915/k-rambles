Busan Transit Cards Are Simpler Than They Look — Here's All You Need to Know

T-money, Cashbee, WOWPASS — where to buy and recharge each one, how refunds work, and exactly how transfer discounts play out.

Last checked September 12, 2026 — fees and policies can change by card issuer or city rule.

The T-money Travel Card, designed for foreign visitors — it works exactly like the standard card on every bus, subway, and village bus nationwide.

You can pay cash on buses and the subway, but a transit card is a lot easier — no counting change, and transfer discounts apply automatically. If you're in Busan for a day or more, it's worth getting one.

Which card should you get

Several transit cards work in Busan. Most do roughly the same job, with a few real differences.

WOWPASS, in more detail

WOWPASS actually started as a currency exchange service for foreign travelers — scan your passport at one of roughly 350 unmanned kiosks nationwide and a card comes out instantly, doubling as both a payment card and a transit card.

  • ₩6,000 issuance fee — issued on the spot at a kiosk or partner counter with your passport.
  • Payments: WOWPASS runs on the Mastercard network, so anywhere in Korea that takes Mastercard — which is most shops, restaurants, and cafes — you can just swipe or tap it like a regular card.
  • Recharging: foreign credit cards or cash, at kiosks or in the app.
  • As a transit card: the app's NFC feature converts part of your balance into T-money, so it taps on buses and the subway like a regular transit card (keep it under ₩30,000).
  • At Gimhae Airport: according to WOWPASS's own kiosk map, there's no kiosk at Gimhae Airport. In Busan, kiosks cluster around Seomyeon, Suyeong/Haeundae, and Nampo-dong/Busan Station — install the app before you land so you can check the nearest one.
  • Refunding: clear out your remaining balance at a kiosk or in the app before you leave — check the app for the exact process and any fee.
Your payment balance and your transit balance are completely separate

WOWPASS's general payment (Mastercard) function and its transit (T-money) function are two separate balances. Money you load at a kiosk or in the app only funds the payment side — to tap on buses and the subway, you have to separately convert part of it to T-money in the app. Skip that step and the card won't work on a bus no matter how much payment balance you have, because the payment balance cannot be used as a transit card.

Refunds vary by card — and by store

Busan subway stations don't do refunds

Seoul subway stations refund up to ₩50,000, but Busan subway stations don't handle refunds at all. In Busan, you'll need a convenience store.

T-money's cap depends on which convenience store you go to — GS25 and 7-Eleven cap it at ₩20,000, while CU and emart24 go up to ₩30,000. Cashbee/EZL is the same everywhere — up to ₩20,000 regardless of chain. (Seoul subway stations refund up to ₩50,000 regardless of card. Busan subway stations don't refund at all.)

Get your refund before you leave Korea — the fee is the same ₩500 everywhere.

Don't mix up these two numbers

The number on a bus terminal or subway gate screen isn't always your "balance." You don't need to read Korean — just memorize the shape of these two words.

Frequently asked questions

  • How do I check my balance? Bus terminals show it on screen ("잔액" = balance). Subway gates display it when you tap, and station recharge machines show it too. You can also ask a convenience store clerk by saying "티머니 잔액 확인해 주세요" (Ti-meo-ni jan-aek hwa-gin-hae ju-se-yo — "Please check my T-money balance").
  • Can I use the card right after buying it? Buying and recharging are separate steps. Load ₩10,000–20,000 as soon as you buy one.
  • Can I recharge with a foreign credit card? T-money and Cashbee only take cash. WOWPASS accepts foreign cards at kiosks and in its app.
  • What if my balance runs low? Check it regularly, and keep some cash as backup — some buses these days don't take cash at all.
  • Do I need to tap when I get off the bus? Yes — it's not a second charge, it registers your exit so the transfer window can be calculated. Always tap out.
  • Can two people ride on one card? On a bus, hold up two fingers to the driver, say "두 명이요" (du myeong-i-yo — "two people"), and tap the card twice to cover both fares. But tapping out doesn't earn the second rider a transfer discount, even if they tap out too. On the subway, each person needs their own card or ticket.
  • Can I buy a card at the airport? There are CU stores in Gimhae Airport's international terminal — ask there first when you land. We couldn't confirm whether they actually carry transit cards, though (inquiries didn't get a clear answer). If they're out, any convenience store in the city — Busan Station or Seomyeon — will have them. There's no WOWPASS kiosk at Gimhae Airport — they cluster in Busan around Seomyeon, Suyeong/Haeundae, and Nampo-dong/Busan Station, so install the app ahead of time and check the nearest one.
  • Where can I use it? Buses, the subway, and the Gimhae Airport light rail all accept it.
  • Can I use it in a taxi? Taxis that take T-money have a T-money mark on the outside of the car. Taxi fares run a lot higher than bus or subway fares, though — if your balance is lower than the fare, it's obviously not going through. T-money doesn't auto-recharge, so check your balance before you get in. If you don't see the mark, it's safest to ask the driver "티머니 카드 되나요?" (Ti-meo-ni ka-deu doe-na-yo? — "Does T-money work?") before you get in.

Before-you-go checklist

  • Buy the card
  • Load it with cash
  • Tap in when boarding
  • Tap out when leaving the bus
  • Check your balance now and then
  • Remember the transfer rules
  • Get a refund before you leave Korea
So, which one should you get?

Most places in Korea take credit cards (Visa/Mastercard) just fine. Still, a transit card is far more convenient if you're staying more than a day.

T-money and Cashbee/EZL work almost identically — T-money is just better known.

Want payments and transit handled by one card? Go with WOWPASS.
