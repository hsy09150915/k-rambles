How to Ride a Taxi in Busan Without the Hassle

Which app to install first, what to do when payment won't go through, and how to show your destination even without a shared language — everything that makes taking a taxi in Busan easier.

Last checked September 12, 2026 — app screens and policies can change.

Choosing a taxi app, paying, and checking the fare — those three things are all you need to ride a taxi in Busan without trouble.

First choice — install k.ride

Made by Kakao Mobility — the same company behind Kakao T — specifically for foreign travelers. It dispatches from the same taxi fleet as Kakao T, but you can sign up with a Google, Apple, or email account — no KakaoTalk account needed at all. Installing this before your trip is the simplest way to start.

  • Register an overseas-issued card in the app to pay automatically.
  • Supports English, Japanese, and Chinese (Simplified/Traditional) interfaces, and both destination search and chat with your driver are auto-translated across roughly 100 languages.
  • Besides standard cars, you can choose a large (minivan) or premium vehicle option.
  • No extra surcharge on top — it runs on the same metered fare as Kakao T.

Since launching in June 2024, it has passed 600,000 cumulative rides and keeps growing.

Second choice — Uber

Uber operates in Busan too — according to Uber's own list of official service areas, coverage now reaches most of the country, including 7 metropolitan cities: Seoul, Busan, Daegu, Incheon, Gwangju, Daejeon, and Ulsan. In practice it dispatches licensed taxis, so it draws from the same fleet as k.ride and Kakao T.

It generally follows the metered fare and, like k.ride and Kakao T, adds no fixed extra surcharge. That said, Uber Taxi in Korea has drivers enter the metered fare into the app manually rather than syncing it automatically — there are occasional user reports of mismatched charges from entry mistakes, so it's worth checking that the meter amount matches what you're charged when you get out.

If you already have an Uber account, you can just log in and use it — no Korean phone number or separate signup needed.

Third choice — Kakao T, if you have a KakaoTalk account

Kakao T is the most-used domestic app in Busan, so it tends to find a driver fastest. But a fresh install only shows one login option — "Log in with KakaoTalk" — there's no way to sign up directly with email or a Google/Apple account. In other words, you need a KakaoTalk account first to use Kakao T.

If you already have a KakaoTalk account, just use it. If you don't and still want one, the signup screen's country-code list includes most countries in the world, so you can sign up with an overseas number. (Verification texts don't always arrive depending on your carrier — if it fails, try switching carriers or waiting and trying again.)

Destination search works whether you type in English or Korean, regardless of the app's display language. You can also switch the interface itself to English from Profile > Language.

Try taxi payment in this order

Most Korean taxis have a standard card payment terminal built into the car — even if you haven't registered a card in an app, there's not much to worry about once you're in. Still, knowing the order helps you avoid getting stuck.

  • First — pay through the taxi app: register a card in k.ride or Kakao T and it charges automatically when you get out. If registration doesn't work, switch to "Pay the driver" on the fare screen, and you can pay by card or cash directly in the car after you arrive.
  • Second — a T-money or Cashbee card: tap it on the terminal in the car. Not every car supports this, so check the section below.
  • Third — an overseas-issued Visa or Mastercard: insert or tap it on the car's card terminal. Unlike app registration, this side usually goes through without trouble.
  • Fourth — cash: keep a little on hand as a last resort.

Can you ride with a T-money or Cashbee card?

Taxis that accept T-money have a T-money mark on the outside. Check for the mark before you get in, and it's safer to also ask the driver "티머니 카드 되나요?" (pronunciation: Ti-meo-ni ka-deu doe-na-yo?) — "Does T-money work?" — before you ride.

Busan's own transit-card infrastructure was originally built around Cashbee (Mybi), so Cashbee tends to work a bit more reliably here than T-money. Card payment still isn't guaranteed either way, so it's worth carrying a little cash just in case.

Where to buy and recharge a transit card itself is covered in detail in Busan Transit Cards Are Simpler Than They Look — Here's All You Need to Know

If you called a ride through k.ride, Uber, or Kakao T, your destination goes straight to the driver's terminal, so you won't need this. Use it when you flag a taxi on the street, or want to confirm the route with the driver directly.

How to show your destination without sharing a language

Search in English
Clear the search
Show it as-is
  1. Keep your map app (Naver Map or Kakao Map) set to English, search your destination in English, and pick the right result from the list.
  2. Selecting a place opens its detail screen, and the Korean name disappears — don't stop here. Tap the X button on the right side of the search bar to clear the search term.
  3. Once you clear the search term, it goes back to the map view, and the pin now shows both English (top) and Korean (below) place names.
  4. Just show that screen to the driver — they only need to read the Korean underneath.
Changing the language setting itself relaunches the app and resets the screen — so it's much simpler to search in English mode from the start, then just clear the search term to reveal the bilingual map.

To avoid getting overcharged

  • Check that the meter turns on as soon as you get in — if it doesn't, ask the driver to turn it on.
  • Checking your expected route on a map app before you leave helps you notice if the driver deliberately takes the long way. Saying, with a smile, "돌아가지 말아주세요" (pronunciation: Do-ra-ga-ji ma-ra-ju-se-yo) — "Please don't take a longer route" — as you set off helps on its own.
  • If it feels like you're going a different way than your map app shows mid-ride, show the driver your map screen and ask "길 맞아요?" (pronunciation: Gil ma-ja-yo?) — "Is this the right way?" — asking out of genuine curiosity rather than an accusing tone is enough.
  • If something goes wrong, call the 1330 Korea Travel Hotline (support in foreign languages) or scan the QR code posted inside the taxi to report it directly.
  • Always get a receipt — it's your only evidence if a problem comes up later.

Late-night taxi tips

The late-night surcharge applies from 11 PM to 4 AM. But standard taxis and deluxe/large taxis are handled completely differently.

Deluxe and large taxis have no late-night surcharge at all, but their base fare and per-distance/time rates are set higher than standard taxis to begin with.

Leaving Busan — the out-of-city surcharge

Standard taxis leaving Busan city limits add another 30% surcharge. If it overlaps with late-night hours, it climbs to 50% during 24:00–02:00, and up to 40% during the other late-night hours (23:00–24:00, 02:00–04:00).

Tour taxis run on an entirely different fare system (often time-based), so it's worth checking separately before you ride. These hours are also harder to catch a taxi in, so calling one through an app ahead of time beats waiting on the street.

Frequently asked questions

  • Which taxi should I call if I have a lot of luggage? Choose k.ride's large (minivan) option, or select "Venti" in Kakao T, and you'll get a car with a bigger trunk. Useful when you have more people or bags.
  • Can I leave k.ride, Uber, and Kakao T all running at once? Yes, that's fine. Keep several open and take whichever finds a driver first, or whichever fare works out better.
  • How do I get a receipt? Ask the card terminal to print one when you get out, or check your ride history inside the Kakao T app.

Before you get in — checklist

  • Sign up for k.ride first (and Kakao T too, if you already have a KakaoTalk account)
  • Try registering a card in your taxi app
  • If that fails, remember how to switch to "Pay the driver"
  • Carry a T-money/Cashbee card, an overseas card, and some cash as backup
  • Search your destination in English, then clear the search term to show the bilingual map
  • Make it a habit to check that the meter turns on as soon as you get in
So, what should you actually prepare?

Install k.ride first — you can use the same taxi fleet without ever needing a KakaoTalk account.

If you already have an Uber account, that's an immediate alternative too — it runs on the same metered fare, so it's not necessarily pricier.

If you already have a KakaoTalk account, Kakao T is also a solid option that finds rides quickly — but it's not worth creating an account just for this.

If the language barrier worries you most, just remember one trick: search in English, then clear the search term to show the bilingual map.
