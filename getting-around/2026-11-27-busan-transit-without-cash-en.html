How to Ride Busan Buses and the Subway Without Cash: Payment App QR, Foreign Cards, Cashless Buses

Until now in Busan, if you didn't have a Korean transit card like T-money, you had to pay cash on both buses and the subway. Topping up a physical transit card also took cash.

That changed in autumn 2026. City buses now take QR payments from home payment apps, and you can buy subway tickets with a foreign card. At the same time, some buses stopped taking cash altogether.

As of November 27, 2026 — some of these are pilot programs and may change.

At a glance
  • On Busan city buses, you can pay with a payment app by scanning the QR code inside the bus (see section 2).
  • On the Busan subway, you can buy a single-journey ticket from the ticket machine with a card issued outside Korea. Some stations get this from December 2026 (see section 4).
  • You can't buy tickets for the light rail from Gimhae Airport (김해공항) with a card issued outside Korea. You buy them with cash at the machine (see section 4).
  • With these three methods, you get no transfer discount when you change between buses, the subway and the light rail.
  • The most convenient option is still a Korean transit card like T-money. You buy a physical card at a convenience store with cash, top it up and tap it on the reader. If you have an iPhone, install the MobileTmoney app and tap your phone on the reader (see section 5).

Examples of Southeast Asian payment apps linked to Korea's ZeroPay. Any app linked to Alipay+, UnionPay or WeChat Pay, as shown on the QR sticker on Busan buses, works. Apple Pay is what you use to top up the MobileTmoney app on an iPhone (see section 5).

2. Paying by QR on city buses with your home payment app

Since September 21, 2026, Busan city buses have accepted ZeroPay (제로페이) QR payments. ZeroPay is Korea's public QR payment system, and it is linked with many payment apps from other countries.

So you can pay your bus fare with the payment app you use at home, without installing a Korean app or buying a transit card. The app converts the currency automatically when it takes the money.

Which apps work

The sticker on the bus shows WeChat Pay, UnionPay and Alipay+. You can pay with Alipay and with payment apps from many countries that are linked to Alipay+.

Some apps need identity verification or a setting changed before they work abroad. Check your app before you leave.

How to pay

  1. Get on the bus and open your payment app.
  2. Scan the ZeroPay QR code posted inside the bus.
  3. Tap the pay button and the payment is done.
  4. Show the payment-complete screen to the driver.

QR payment is a substitute for cash, so unlike a transit card there's no discount when you transfer. If you get off the bus and take the subway or another bus, you pay the full fare again.

3. Buses that don't take cash: 35 routes

From October 1 to December 31, 2026, Busan is running a pilot of 'cashless buses' (현금 없는 버스) on some city bus routes. That's 542 buses on 35 routes, about 20% of Busan's city buses.

These buses have no cash fare box, so you can't pay with bills or coins. The buses carry a cashless bus sign.

Bank transfer needs a Korean bank account, so it's hard for travelers. Have either a Korean transit card or a home payment app ready. QR stickers are on every Busan city bus.

Busan plans to review the pilot, make improvements and then extend cashless buses to all city buses.

4. Buying subway tickets with a foreign card

At station ticket machines

Since October 21, 2026, you can buy single-journey tickets by card at ticket machines in Busan subway stations.

For foreign cards, Visa, Mastercard and JCB work, as do mobile payments like Apple Pay, WeChat Pay and Alipay.

A single-journey ticket from the machine comes out as a paper ticket with a QR code printed on it. Hold the QR part to the QR reader at the gate to go through.

Stations where it doesn't work yet

At the 6 stations at the southwest end of Line 1 (Dongmae (동매) to Dadaepo Beach (다대포해수욕장)) and at every Line 4 station, card payment at the ticket machines is planned to start in December 2026 because of machine work. If you start from one of these stations, buy your ticket in the app below.

In the Busan Metro app

You can also buy tickets with a foreign card (Visa, Mastercard, JCB) in the Busan Metro app (부산도시철도) from Busan Transportation Corporation (부산교통공사). Foreigners can use it without signing up, with just an email address and a verification code.

Fares are split into Zone 1 and Zone 2 by distance. App ticket fares are the same as transit card fares. If you use the app without an account, the adult fare applies.

Tickets, 1-day passes and 3-day passes are for Busan subway Lines 1–4 only. You can't use them on buses, the Donghae Line (동해선) or the Busan-Gimhae Light Rail, and there's no transfer discount to buses.

The light rail from Gimhae Airport is different

The Busan-Gimhae Light Rail (부산김해경전철), which links Gimhae Airport station and Sasang (사상) station, is run by a different company from the Busan subway. So the card payment at ticket machines above doesn't apply to the light rail, and you buy light rail single-journey tickets with cash at the machine. Busan Metro app tickets don't work on it either.

Korean transit cards like T-money work anywhere in the country, and you get the transfer discount between the light rail and the Busan subway. If you get off the light rail at Sasang and change to subway Line 2 within 30 minutes, you get the discount.

Light rail single-journey tickets, subway machine tickets and Busan Metro app tickets have no transfer discount, so you pay the light rail fare and the subway fare separately, twice. From Gimhae Airport to Seomyeon (서면) or Haeundae (해운대) station, the difference looks like this.

5. When a Korean transit card (like T-money) is still better

QR payment and foreign card tickets are handy when you don't have a transit card, but if you transfer a lot or travel with children, a transit card is much cheaper.

City regular bus, adultFareTransfer discount
Korean transit card1,550 wonYes
Cash1,700 wonNo
Home payment app QR1,700 wonNo

Changing from the subway to a bus

If you ride the subway (Zone 1) with a transit card and change to a regular bus within 30 minutes of getting off, the transfer discount brings the two fares to 1,600 won in total. You pay only the Zone 1 subway fare (1,600 won) and nothing extra for the bus.

The same trip with a machine ticket and a bus QR payment means paying 1,700 won and 1,700 won separately: 3,400 won.

Traveling with children

Children aged 6 to 12 ride Busan buses and the subway free if their transit card is registered as a child card. Buy a card at a convenience store and register the child's date of birth, and it works right away.

With bus QR payment, children pay 1,700 won too.

Using your iPhone as a transit card

If you have an iPhone, the MobileTmoney app lets you use it like a transit card. Tap 'Foreigner' on the app's first screen to create a T-money and add it to Apple Wallet without signing up.

You top it up in the app with Apple Pay. For foreign cards, Mastercard, American Express, UnionPay and JCB work; Visa doesn't yet.

App Store

A T-money made this way works on Busan buses and the subway like a physical transit card, with the same transfer discounts. You need an iPhone XS or later and iOS 17.2 or later.

Physical transit cards

You buy a physical T-money card at a convenience store and top it up with cash. Where to buy one, how to top up, refunds and transfer rules are covered in our Busan Transit Card Guide, and how to get on and off a bus in our How to Ride the Bus in Korea.

7. In Seoul and Jeju

Many travelers go on to Seoul or Jeju after Busan, so here is a short summary of how to pay in those two cities too. What works differs by city.

MethodBusanSeoulJeju

Pay by QR on city buses with a home payment appYesNoYes
Buy subway tickets with a foreign cardYesYesN/A

In Jeju, Visa is the only foreign card you can tap to ride. For subway tickets, use station machines and the Busan Metro app in Busan, and the new machines on Lines 1–8 in Seoul.

Seoul

Since March 17, 2026, the new transit card machines at 273 stations on Seoul subway Lines 1–8 let you buy single-journey tickets with a foreign card (Visa, Mastercard, etc.), and buy or top up short-term Climate Card (기후동행카드) passes (1, 2, 3, 5 and 7 days). The Climate Card is a Seoul-only pass for unlimited rides on Seoul's subway and buses for the length of the pass.

Paying with a foreign card adds a service fee of 3.7% on average.

You can't tap a foreign card at the gates or on buses to ride yet. Seoul plans to start with city buses and airport buses in 2027 and expand by 2030.

Jeju

Since August 2025, Jeju city buses have let you tap a foreign Visa card on the reader to ride. It was the first place in Korea to do this.

Jeju buses have also taken ZeroPay QR payments since August 2024. You can pay with home payment apps like Alipay and WeChat Pay.

One transit card covers all three cities

A T-money card bought in Busan works as-is in Seoul and Jeju. So does iPhone MobileTmoney. If you're visiting several cities, getting one transit card is the simplest choice.
