Busan SIM & eSIM Guide — The 3 Carriers, Plus How to Pay Less

Everything in one place: SK, KT, and LG U+ official plans, the real difference between a physical SIM and an eSIM, how to activate without an airport visit, and how to pay less with a third-party eSIM.

Last checked September 13, 2026 — carrier plans and promo prices can change anytime.

With an eSIM, a few taps in the app before you land is all it takes — a physical SIM still means a stop at the airport.

Short version: for a short trip, SK, KT, and LG U+ are priced almost identically. The real differences come down to whether you have to visit the airport and what extra perks you get — not price. And there's also a much cheaper option than any of the big three: third-party eSIMs. Here's the full breakdown, in order.

So, which one should you actually get?

For a short trip where you only need data, a third-party eSIM (Klook or KKday) is the cheapest and easiest option.

If you need a Korean phone number for calls and don't want to stop at the airport, an official SK Telecom eSIM (or a third-party eSIM on the SK network) is basically your only option.

If you're traveling as a group or need to connect several devices at once, consider a pocket wifi router — and here too, Klook or KKday can be cheaper than booking direct from the carrier's official site.

Physical SIM vs. eSIM: What's Actually Different

This is the part people mix up most. A physical SIM card (USIM) always requires an airport pickup with all three carriers, whether it's data-only or includes calling — that makes sense, since you need to physically receive a card. If your goal is "get data ready without going anywhere near the airport," you need an eSIM from the start, not a physical SIM.

A data-only eSIM can be completed entirely online with all three carriers. Add calling and texting, though, and it's a different story — KT and LG U+ still require in-person ID verification at the airport even for an eSIM, while SK is the only one that lets you verify remotely using the voucher emailed to you.

Need calling but don't want to lose time at the airport?

SK is basically your only option. Even if you buy an eSIM from KT or LG U+, you'll still have to go through the airport roaming center to activate calling.

Staying 60+ Days?

If you're planning 60–90 days for study abroad, a working holiday, or a longer posting, LG U+ ends up noticeably pricier than SK or KT. It won't matter for a short trip, but it's worth knowing if you're staying for months.

Do You Even Need a Pocket Wifi Router?

Pricing is identical across all three carriers at ₩4,400/day — there's essentially no competition here. Since most smartphones now support eSIM, an eSIM is far more convenient than a router if you're traveling solo (no airport pickup or return, no deposit needed either). A router really only makes sense in two situations:

  • Group travel, where several people need to share one data connection
  • You need to connect multiple devices at once — an older phone that doesn't support eSIM, a laptop, and so on
No credit card for the deposit? Go with LG U+

SK and KT only accept credit cards — foreign-issued Amex, Samsung Card, Hana Card, mobile pay, and debit cards are all excluded. LG U+ is the only one that accepts a debit card for the deposit — a real difference if you're traveling without a credit card.

Comparing the Extra Perks

  • SK — A free T-money card and a palace admission ticket with any SIM/eSIM purchase, no conditions attached, plus a discount coupon book for Everland, Lotte World, and Busan attractions like X the SKY and Busan Tower. The richest perk package of the three.
  • KT — Pick the "Data & Travel" plan (0 call minutes, data plus a T-money card bundle) and it actually ends up cheaper than the plain data-only plan, with a T-money card thrown in.
  • LG U+ — Only gives you a T-money card if you buy 3+ days and pick it up at the airport — the strictest condition of the three.

For reference, a regular T-money card costs about ₩3,000 at any convenience store (GS25, CU, etc.) — so that's roughly the real value of the "free" perk, not enough on its own to swing your decision. That said, SK stacks on a palace ticket and a discount coupon book on top, so its overall perk package is still a bit more generous than the other two.

How Much Cheaper Are Third-Party eSIMs?

In Korea, only three companies actually build cell towers and buy spectrum from the government to run their own network infrastructure: SK Telecom, KT, and LG U+. Most budget carriers (MVNOs, 알뜰폰) are either subsidiaries of the big three or lease network capacity from them under contract, since they can't afford the massive capital cost of a spectrum auction and building out infrastructure.

Third-party SIM/eSIM products sold to tourists — the ones you'll find on Klook, KKday, and similar sites — follow the exact same structure. They don't run their own network; they're reselling capacity on the SKT, KT, or LG U+ network. In other words, call quality and speed are identical to the official plans — only the price is different.
