Amidong Tombstone Village in Busan: What to Know Before You Go

Right next door to Gamcheon, with tombstones still built into its stairs and walls. People actually live here, so it calls for a different kind of visit.

Last checked September 5, 2026 — visiting details can change, so treat older information with caution.

Steps and a wall built from old tombstones and altar stones — right next to it is a house people still live in. Photo: Busan Tourism Archive (Busan Tourism Organization / Busan Metropolitan City), KOGL Type 1.

The real story

The area around San 19, Amidong was a Japanese cemetery during the colonial period. After liberation, it sat abandoned for about five years — until the Korean War changed everything.

Refugees poured into Busan faster than the city could ever house them, and there was no building material to be had. People who needed a roof that same day laid planks over the abandoned graves and started building homes, using the tombstones and altar stones as steps, walls, and foundations. Remains that turned up in the process weren't simply discarded — they were given a proper rite, then moved to a nearby temple, according to local accounts.

A mural, not a historical photo — painted in the village to commemorate the refugee-settlement era. Photo: Busan Tourism Archive, KOGL Type 1.

How it connects to Gamcheon

It's separated from Gamcheon Culture Village by a single road. Both neighborhoods trace back to Korean War refugees settling the hillside, but Amidong isn't on Gamcheon's official stamp-tour ("passport") route — it's a separate stop you have to seek out on your own.

Confirmed via Visit Busan (official Busan tourism information), as of September 5, 2026.

What's still visible today

Steps, walls, and house foundations throughout the village still have the original tombstones and altar stones set into them. Some carry the crest of a Japanese family, still legible if you look closely.

A tombstone carved with a Japanese family crest, still set into the corner of a wall. Photo: Busan Tourism Archive, KOGL Type 1.

Climb to the observation deck above the village and the hillside houses and the old downtown skyline beyond come into view together. There are murals here and there too, but unlike Gamcheon, the whole village wasn't dressed up for tourists.

What to keep in mind when you visit

This is where Amidong differs most from Gamcheon. Gamcheon has cafes, gift shops, and an information center — it was built to welcome tourists from day one. Amidong isn't like that. It's an ordinary residential neighborhood where people still actually live, and tourism was never the reason it exists.

  • Keep your voice down and move quietly through the alleys.
  • Don't look into or photograph people's gates or yards. These are actual homes.
  • Stick to the alleys and the viewpoint, and stay out of anything that looks like private property.
  • Don't move or climb on the tombstones and altar stones — they're heritage, and still part of someone's home.
  • Avoid visiting early in the morning or late in the evening — that's when residents are living their daily lives.
One more reason to keep it quiet

The neighborhood's future hasn't been a quiet topic either. In 2021, residents pushed back against the Seo-gu district office's plan to build a monorail parking lot, demanding that their right to stay be guaranteed. This isn't just a backdrop for photos — it's a place where real lives are still at stake.

Money Today and Nocut News, June 2021.

Learn more at the Ami Culture Learning Center first

If you want a deeper, no-pressure look at the village's history, stop by the Ami Culture Learning Center first. It's a place where you can take in Amidong's history through photos and materials, without having to walk into the alleys where people actually live.

Getting there

There are two ways in — walk the whole way, or ride the village bus for one stop.

Route 1 — On foot

Take Metro Line 1 to Toseong Station Exit 8 and just walk up from there.

About a 20-minute walk

Route 2 — Village bus

Come out of Toseong Station Exit 6, turn right, and walk about 120m to the Pusan National University Hospital stop. Take village bus Saha-gu 1-1 or Seo-gu 2 (사하구1-1, 서구2) and get off at the Sansang Church (Tombstone Cultural Village) stop.

Fare about ₩1,600

Parking — the Amigol public parking lot is available.
