Igidae Coastal Walk — A Volcanic Cliff Trail That Only Opened in 1993

A walking trail along sea cliffs with views of Gwangan Bridge and Haeundae. It was a military zone for decades and closed to the public, and the volcanic rock formed 80 million years ago is still there.

As of October 7, 2026 — please check opening hours, closures and prices before you go.

Igidae at night — lights glow along the bridge and boardwalk between the cliffs.

What Is Igidae?

Igidae is a stretch of sea cliffs on the shore of Yongho-dong, Nam-gu, Busan. The trail along it is the Igidae Coastal Walk, part of the Busan National Geopark. There is no admission fee, and it is open year-round.

The Name and the History — Two Gisaeng, and a Coast Closed for Decades

Where does the name come from? Igidae (二妓臺) means "the place of two gisaeng" — gisaeng were professional female entertainers of the Joseon era. The Dongnae Yeongji, a local gazetteer written in 1850, says the spot was called Igidae because "two gisaeng are buried above." Another story says a Gyeongsang Left Naval Commander (the regional navy chief) enjoyed feasts here with two gisaeng. A different account says that during the Imjin War (the Japanese invasion), a righteous gisaeng threw herself into the sea holding a drunken Japanese general, so the place was first called Uigidae (義妓臺, "the place of the righteous gisaeng"), which later became Igidae. Which version is right has never been settled.

A place people could not enter. During the Japanese colonial period, Japanese troops are said to have built coastal guard barracks and positions along this shore. Afterward it stayed a restricted military zone for a long time, closed to civilians, and it only opened to the public in 1993. There was also a copper mine here, worked during the colonial period; it is said to run 550 m horizontally and 380 m vertically.

What the closure preserved

Because access was restricted for so long, the coast saw less development, and several guides say the cliffs and rocky terrain remain relatively intact.

What to See Along the Way — Volcanic Rock and Wave-Carved Landforms

The rock at Igidae formed from volcanic activity about 80 million years ago, in the late Cretaceous. You can see layers of rock built up from lava and volcanic ash, and some guides say traces of at least three eruptions can be seen here. Over a very long time, waves carved this rock into the cliffs you see today.

The seasons. Guides describe flowers in spring, sea breezes in summer, silver grass and sunsets in autumn, and clear views in winter. Some visitors report yellow daffodils at Oryukdo Sunrise Park around mid-March. The wind off the sea is often strong, so bring a light jacket.

This is not a loop but a one-way trail. The Korea Tourism Organization describes it in the order below; you can walk it in reverse too.

There are not many facilities along the way, so bring water. Sneakers are recommended.

You can walk it at night too

About 1.26 km of the trail is lit, from sunset until 10 p.m. (since January 2024). Outside the lit section it is dark, so after dark stick to the safe stretches and do not push it.

The Oryukdo Skywalk has different hours

The Skywalk is open daily 09:00–18:00. If you plan to walk late in the evening, see the Skywalk first, in daylight. You have to wear shoe covers over your shoes, and it may close in heavy rain or wind.

Oryukdo Skywalk

A U-shaped glass bridge at the end of Oryukdo Sunrise Park. It juts out about 15 m from a 35 m-high cliff, with the sea visible under your feet. This is known as the point where the East Sea and the South Sea meet, so the Haeparang Trail, which runs along the east coast, starts here, and the Namparang Trail, which follows the south coast, ends here. Oryukdo are six rocky islets just offshore. The name means "five or six islands": the island nearest the shore, Usakdo, looks like one island at low tide and like two (Bangpaeseom and Solseom) at high tide, so people counted either five or six. An old record also says it looks like five peaks from some spots and six from others.

See It From the Sea — Haeundae Cruise

Instead of walking, you can look up at the Igidae cliffs and Oryukdo from a boat. The Oryukdo Marine Tour from Haeundae Cruise (Mipo Pier, Haeundae) is described as passing Dongbaekseom → Gwangan Bridge → Igidae → Oryukdo. In the evening there is also a Gwangan Bridge Night Tour.

Check Before You Go

Parts of the trail may be closed for maintenance or safety checks. Before you visit, check with the Nam-gu Office Parks and Greenery Division (051-607-4544) or the Igidae Park management office (051-607-6398).

K-Rambles Tip

If it is your first time, start at Oryukdo Sunrise Park and see the Skywalk first, then walk in toward Eoulmadang if you have time. You do not have to walk the whole way. You can also use only half your energy and turn back, keeping the return trip in mind.
