Gwangalli at Night — Drone Show Times, Where to Watch, and What to Know Before You Go

Gwangalli is better known for its nights than its days. After sunset the bridge lights up, and on drone show nights pictures appear in the sky. Go without knowing the times or where to stand and you may see little more than the backs of heads, so here is what you need.

As of October 8, 2026 — please check show dates and times on the official site before you go.

Gwangalli Beach — Gwangandaegyo Bridge stands beyond the sand. At night the bridge is lit up.

Gwangandaegyo Bridge — Better at Night

Gwangalli Beach is about 1.4 km of sand, with Gwangandaegyo Bridge spanning the sea right in front of it. The bridge lighting is designed to show weather data such as fine-dust levels and rainfall as light, and the display changes with the seasons, holidays and local festivals. In 2026 it won second place in the Main Award category of the LUCI (Lighting Urban Community International) City and Lighting Awards.

Gwangandaegyo is a road bridge for cars, so you cannot normally walk across it. Parts of it have been opened to pedestrians during events, but on an ordinary day you view it from the beach or the waterfront park.

Gwangandaegyo Bridge lit up — the colours and effects change with the weather, the season and events.

The Drone Show — Times and How to Check the Schedule

This is the Gwangalli M Drone Light Show, held at Gwangalli Beach. Drones draw pictures in the night sky above the bridge. The theme changes every time, and has included cartoon characters, holidays and traditional stories.

ItemDetails
DayMostly Saturdays (some weeks are skipped)
Times (Oct–Feb)19:00 and 21:00 (twice a night)
Times (Mar–Sep)20:00 and 22:00 (twice a night)
LengthAbout 12 minutes
AdmissionFree; you can watch from anywhere on the beach
CancellationRain, strong wind, typhoons or communication problems can cancel or delay the show, even on the day or the day before

The drone show — drones draw pictures in the night sky above the bridge. The theme is different every time.

Dates and themes change from month to month. For the schedule at the time you read this, check the English page of the official site. The times change with the season, so treat the times in the table above as a guide only.

It gets colder than you expect after dark because of the sea wind. Bring a warm jacket and a mat to sit on, and a power bank if you will be taking photos. Claiming a spot about 30 minutes before the show starts leaves some room to spare.

Millac The Market — Eat, Drink and Wander

A complex of shops and cultural space right next to Millak Waterside Park. It opened in July 2022 and covers about 2,330 pyeong (roughly 7,700 m²) from the first basement level to the second floor. There is no entry fee, and it is open daily 10:00–24:00 (it varies by shop).

Who Goes There?

It is described as a place popular with people in their 20s and 30s, and also with families, couples and groups of friends. Many foreign visitors go too. One report says about half of the visitors to the Olive Young store here are foreign, and foreign card spending in the area more than doubled in a year.

Dishes That May Be Unfamiliar

Gopchang is cow's small intestine. Grilled, it is chewy on the outside and rich inside. The texture is different from ordinary meat and can take getting used to at first. Share one plate and try it.

Hanwoo is beef from Korean-raised cattle. It is so fresh and tasty that even Koreans crave it. It is expensive and not eaten often, so people usually eat cheaper imported beef day to day.

Bringing your own alcohol to drink at Millak Waterside Park is reported to be restricted there, so check the signs on site.

Getting There — Subway and a Short Walk

Take Subway Line 2 to Gwangan Station or Geumnyeonsan Station and walk to the beach. From Geumnyeonsan Station, take the road between Exits 1 and 3. From Gwangan Station, take the road between Exits 3 and 5. The walk takes under 10 minutes.

Busan Fireworks Festival — Steel Yourself

When people in Korea name the big fireworks festivals, they mean Seoul and Busan. Seoul's is the Seoul World Fireworks Festival at Yeouido; Busan's is the Busan Fireworks Festival, held over the sea off Gwangalli and the bridge. In Busan the bridge is the backdrop, and fireworks are also launched from Igidae and Dongbaekseom.

The date changes every year. It used to be held in October, and in recent years it has been on a Saturday in November: November 9 in 2024 and November 15 in 2025 (November 7 in 2026, for reference). So don't rely on this year's date; check the year's schedule on the English page of the official site.

Busan Fireworks Festival — fireworks rise behind Gwangandaegyo Bridge.

Busan Fireworks Festival official site (English)
Be Prepared

Reports say about 1.17 million people came in 2025. Around a million people attend each year. The beach, the beach road and the subway stations are on a completely different scale from an ordinary drone show night. Safety fences and a crowd-capacity system were used, and the subway ran many extra trains, because it gets that crowded. Spots on the beach fill up hours ahead, and after the show it takes a long time just to get into a station. Don't bring a car.
