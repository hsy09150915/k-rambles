How to Get to Yangdong Village in Gyeongju: Bus 203 Times, Tickets and a Day Trip Plan

Yangdong Village (양동마을) is a UNESCO World Heritage Site: a Joseon-era village of yangban (aristocratic) clans that people still live in today, much as they always have.

This guide is for travelers without a car: where to catch bus 203 and its timetable, tickets, the village guided tour, and a day plan that adds Oksan Seowon (옥산서원).

As of November 23, 2026 — bus times and fares can change.

1. At a glance

Yangdong Village lies north of downtown Gyeongju, so it is a little tricky to reach without a car. Knowing one city bus, number 203, gets you right to the village.

2. What is Yangdong Village?

Two noble (yangban) clans of the Joseon era, the Son clan of Gyeongju (경주 손씨) and the Yi clan of Yeogang (여강 이씨), have lived here together for close to 600 years. Joseon was Korea's last royal dynasty, which ruled from 1392 to 1910.

Tile-roofed houses stand high on the hillsides and thatched-roof houses sit lower down. More than 50 old houses over 200 years old remain, and in 2010 the village became a UNESCO World Heritage Site, "Historic Villages of Korea," together with Hahoe Village (하회마을) in Andong.

It is not a folk village built for show. People still live here, so you cannot enter ordinary homes, and even at houses designated as Treasures (a state heritage designation) you can only see the outer areas, not the inner quarters.

These are the four best-known houses. The village spreads across the hills, so seeing it all takes about 2 hours.

3. Taking city bus 203

Bus 203 is a city bus that starts at Gyeongju Intercity Bus Terminal (경주시외버스터미널), stops at Yangdong Village and continues on toward Oksan Seowon. The Express Bus Terminal (고속버스터미널) is right next to the Intercity Bus Terminal, so if you arrive by express bus, just walk over and change.

Where to get on
The first stop is the city bus stop in front of the Intercity Bus Terminal. It is about a 12-minute walk from Hwangnidan-gil (황리단길).

Where to get off
There are two stops near the village. Get off at Yangdong Folk Village (양동민속마을), right in front of the village, and the ticket office is a 3-minute walk.

Yangdong Village Entrance (양동마을입구), one stop earlier, is on the main road. You can walk to the village from there too, but it takes about 10 minutes longer than from Yangdong Folk Village.

Other buses besides 203 also go there

Buses 200, 205 and 207 also run from the Intercity Bus Terminal to Yangdong Village Entrance (양동마을입구). They run more often than 203, so they are handy when the 203 times do not suit you.

They do not go up to the front of the village, so you get off at Yangdong Village Entrance and walk about 10 minutes more. It is only a little more walking, so it is fine to take whichever bus comes first.

If you arrive at Gyeongju Station by KTX

KTX is Korea's high-speed train. There is no direct bus from KTX Gyeongju Station to Yangdong Village. First take a city bus to the Intercity Bus Terminal, then change to bus 203. Buses from Gyeongju Station to downtown are covered in How to get from Busan to Gyeongju.

If you have big luggage, leave it near the terminal before you go. The village has many hilly paths, so pulling a suitcase around is hard. Places to leave luggage are in Gyeongju luggage storage.

4. Taking a taxi

From downtown Gyeongju to Yangdong Village takes 20–30 minutes by car. If the bus times do not suit you, or you are a group of 3 or 4, a taxi is a good option.

Gyeongju taxis add a surcharge (bokhap haljeung, 복합할증) when going far from downtown, so the fare is high for the distance. Check the estimated fare in a taxi app such as Kakao T before you ride.

You can also mix the two, taking a taxi there and bus 203 back.

Say this to the taxi driver

양동마을로 가주세요Yang-dong-ma-eul-lo ga-ju-se-yo

If it is hard to say, you can show this screen to the driver. It means "Please take me to Yangdong Village."

Travel time follows Gyeongju City's tourism guide.

The village is open every day of the year. Only the Village Culture Hall (마을 문화관, a free exhibition hall) at the entrance closes on Mondays.

People live in this village

You cannot go inside a house without the owner's permission. If a gate is closed, just walk on by, and keep your voice down near the houses.

Bicycles and pets are not allowed in the village. There are many slopes and dirt paths, so wear comfortable shoes.

7. Staying overnight in Yangdong Village

The village has traditional guesthouses where you stay in thatched-roof or tile-roofed houses that locals live in. If you want to see the quiet village after the day visitors leave and the early morning scenery, staying a night is a good idea.

Hyangdan, a state-designated Treasure, also has rooms you can stay in.

Booking option 1: Airbnb

For international visitors, Airbnb is the easiest. Search for "Yangdong Village" and you will find several stays inside the village, from rooms in thatched-roof houses to whole thatched-roof houses and old family houses. You can pay with an overseas card and book in English.

When we checked in November 2026, prices for one night ranged from around 60,000 won for one room in a thatched-roof house to over 500,000 won for places such as the annex of Hyangdan, a state-designated Treasure. Prices change by date, so check for yourself.

8. What is Oksan Seowon?

Bus 203 continues past Yangdong Village to Oksan Seowon. About 10 minutes further by bus from Yangdong Village, you reach a valley surrounded by mountains and streams, where Oksan Seowon and Dongnakdang House stand.

A seowon was a private school of the Joseon era
Unlike schools set up by the state, a seowon (서원) was a Confucian academy where local scholars honored a teacher they revered and taught students. Study halls stand at the front, and a shrine holding the teacher's memorial tablet stands at the back.

Oksan Seowon (옥산서원)
It was built in 1572 to honor the scholar Yi Eonjeok, who was born in Yangdong Village. His learning was passed on to the great scholar Yi Hwang (Toegye, 퇴계 이황) and became a foundation of Joseon Neo-Confucianism.

In 2019 it was listed as one of the 9 academies of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Seowon, Korean Neo-Confucian Academies." Before that, in 2010, it had already been inscribed as part of "Historic Villages of Korea" together with Yangdong Village.

As you enter, you first see the two-story pavilion Mubyeollu (무변루). It is described as the first seowon building to have a raised wooden floor (numaru). In the lecture hall Guindang (구인당) opposite hangs an "Oksan Seowon" name board written by the famous Joseon calligrapher Kim Jeong-hui (Chusa, 추사 김정희).

Sesimdae Valley (세심대)
Right in front of the academy are wide flat rocks and a clear stream. This is Sesimdae, meaning "a place to wash the mind," where the scholars rested and calmed their minds between studies.

Dongnakdang House (독락당)
The house where Yi Eonjeok lived and studied for about 7 years after leaving his government post. It is about 600 m up the valley from the academy.

Highlights are the pavilion Gyejeong (계정), built on the rocks above the stream, and the wooden lattice window cut into the earthen wall so you can see the stream even while sitting in the room. Descendants still live in the house, so please look around quietly.

Dongnakdang also runs as a gotaek stay (고택스테이): a night in an old family house built as a hanok (traditional Korean house). If you want a quiet day in a mountain valley, staying the night is an option, and you can then see Oksan Seowon and the valley early the next morning with no one around.

Thirteen-story Stone Pagoda at Jeonghyesa Temple Site (정혜사지 십삼층석탑)
About 700 m further north of Dongnakdang, a 5.9 m tall stone pagoda with 13 stories stands in a field. It dates from the Unified Silla period (668–935), and its shape is so rare that it has been designated a National Treasure.

Who will enjoy it

We recommend it to anyone who prefers quiet, out-of-the-way places to crowded downtown sites, and likes scenery where old buildings blend with nature.

If you prefer busy places with lots to see, seeing just Yangdong Village and heading back downtown is plenty.
