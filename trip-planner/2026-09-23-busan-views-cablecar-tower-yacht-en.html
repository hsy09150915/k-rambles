Seeing Busan Without Walking — Cable Car, Observation Tower, Yacht

Busan rewards walking, but sometimes it’s better seen sitting down. From the sky, from a downtown tower, from the water — three completely different angles on the same city.

As of September 2026 — check fares and hours again before you go.

The Busan coastline and skyline as seen from the Songdo Marine Cable Car.

Busan is a walking city. Alleys, coastal paths, markets — a lot of it only shows itself on foot.

But sometimes sitting down is the better view. Looking down at the coastline from the sky, taking in the whole harbor from a tower in the middle of downtown, or looking back up at the city from the water — each gives you something the walking routes simply don’t.

1. From the sky — Songdo Marine Cable Car

A cable car that crosses the Songdo sea at up to 86m above the water. It’s a renewal of Korea’s very first cable car line, which opened in 1964, reborn under the name Busan Air Cruise.

It runs from Songdo Bay Station (171 Songdohaebyeon-ro) to Songdo Sky Park on the Amnam Park side, taking about 10 minutes one way.

AIR CRUISE

The standard cabin, with a solid floor. Easier on you if heights aren’t your thing.

CRYSTAL CRUISE

The glass-floor cabin. Foreign reviewers repeatedly say the extra 5,000 won is well spent.

SKY PARK

The Amnam Park side station. With an observation deck, forest paths, and the Yonggung Suspension Bridge here, it becomes a whole outing rather than just a ride.

How foreign visitors rate it

Of the three places in this article, this one has the most foreign reviews and the strongest ratings. English-language Tripadvisor reviews repeatedly call it the best cable car they’ve ridden and say it’s well worth the money.

One thing to watch: tickets bought online often can’t be used the same day. If you’re heading there today, buy at the on-site counter instead.

Getting there and which station to use

You can board at either station. With a round-trip ticket it makes no difference which end you start from.

1. Songdo Bay Station (lower) — 171 Songdohaebyeon-ro, Seo-gu, Busan
Right at Songdo Beach. Good if you want the beach and the Songdo Cloud Walk (a 365m walkway over the sea with glass-floor sections · free) in the same trip.
From Nampo Station, take bus 6, 7, 30, or 71 and get off at Songdo Beach — about 20 minutes, around KRW 1,600.

2. Songdo Sky Park (upper) — 181 Amnamgongwon-ro, Seo-gu, Busan
The Amnam Park side. This is where you’ll find the park trails, the coastal cliffs, and the Songdo Yonggung Suspension Bridge (a 127m suspension bridge from Amnam Park out to the uninhabited islet of Dongseom · KRW 1,000 entry).
From Nampo Station, take bus 7 or 71 and get off at Amnam Park — about 25 minutes, around KRW 1,600.

Naver Map Google Maps

So which end? Beach-and-sea atmosphere, start at Songdo Beach. Forest paths, cliffs, and the suspension bridge, start at Amnam Park. With a round-trip ticket you can get off at the far end, look around, and ride back — so you end up seeing both sides either way.

Bus routes do get reshuffled. Worth a quick check in a map app before you set out.

2. From downtown — Busan Tower (Yongdusan Park)

The tower structure itself is 120m tall. On top of that, it sits on Yongdusan hill (about 69m above sea level), so the height you actually look out from is greater than the tower figure alone suggests.

Its biggest advantage is location. It’s right beside Nampo-dong, BIFF Square, and Jagalchi Market, so you can ride the escalators up mid-shopping and be back down shortly.

Per Busan City’s official tourism information. Yongdusan Park itself is free — only the tower observatory is ticketed. There is also a projection-mapping fireworks show in the evening.

How foreign visitors rate it

The English-language reviews are solid — several call it unmissable on a Busan trip and the best observatory they’ve been to. That said, there are also reviews noting it underwhelms on an overcast day.

This is one you can happily buy on the spot. Queues don’t get extreme here, so you can check the weather and decide on the day.

Yongdusan Park entrance — 37-30 Yongdusan-gil, Jung-gu, Busan

3. From the water — Songdo yacht tour

A 50-minute yacht tour departing from Songdo Beach. If you rode the cable car earlier and looked down from above, this flips it — now you’re looking up at that same cable car from the water.

The route runs past the Songdo Marine Cable Car, the Amnam Park cliffs, and the Namhang Bridge night view. It’s a double-deck catamaran, so it rocks relatively little, and guiding is available in English, Japanese, Chinese, and Korean.

An honest note — this one is less proven

Unlike the two above, sales on international booking platforms are still low — in the 25-booking range. So we can’t tell you that lots of foreign visitors have been and rated it highly.

On the other hand, Korean blogs carry a fair number of Songdo yacht write-ups. In other words: reasonably known among Koreans, still under the radar for foreign travelers.

Worth considering if crowded Gwangalli yacht tours put you off. If you want the proven option, the two above are the safer bet.

Good to know

· 50 minutes · departs Songdo Beach
· Night sailings include onboard fireworks and a rose giveaway
· Free cancellation up to 6 days before your date
· Sailings can be canceled in bad weather

The short version

  • If you only do one: the Songdo Marine Cable Car. Strongest foreign reviews, and there’s plenty to do at the far end.
  • If you have an awkward gap to fill: Busan Tower. Right in the middle of Nampo-dong, easy to slot in, and fine to buy on the spot.
  • If you want somewhere others skip: the Songdo yacht — bearing in mind foreign reviews are still thin.
  • If you combine them in a day: do Songdo (cable car + yacht) as one leg, then head back to Nampo-dong and finish at Busan Tower.

Fares and hours shift with season and weather. Worth re-checking official channels before you go.
