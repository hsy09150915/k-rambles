Taejongdae, Done Right — From the Danubi Train to Yeongdo Lighthouse and Sinseon Rock

Forest trails, a 100m coastal cliff, Yeongdo Lighthouse, and Sinseon Rock — Busan’s signature coastal scenic spot. Here’s the essentials for getting around a park that’s bigger than it looks.

As of September 22, 2026 — double-check the Danubi Train’s official hours and fares before you visit.

Taejongdae's signature view — blue sea, coastal cliffs, and Yeongdo Lighthouse all at once.

First: Taejongdae is bigger than it looks

Arriving at the Taejongdae entrance doesn’t mean the lighthouse and cliffs are right there.

The loop road inside the park runs about 4.3km, and walking the whole thing takes roughly 2 hours. Strolling the forest trail at your own pace is lovely, but if you’re short on time or not up for the uphill stretches, the Danubi Train is the easier way to go.

K-Rambles Tip

On a first visit, you don’t need to walk the whole loop.

If you’re short on time or the uphill sections feel like too much, take the Danubi Train and hop off at whichever stop you want to explore.

The Danubi Train is a circular tourist train that connects Taejongdae’s main sights along the loop road.

If you plan to get off at several spots and re-board later, get the round-trip (circular) ticket. That lets you hop off at stops like the observatory, Yeongdo Lighthouse, or Taejongsa Temple, look around, and catch the next Danubi Train onward. A one-way ticket, on the other hand, can’t be used to re-board once you get off. On busy days, you may have to wait for the next train.

Per Busan Geopark’s current guide, the route runs: the plaza → Taewon Jagalmadang → Gumyeongsa → the observatory → Yeongdo Lighthouse → Taejongsa Temple → back to the plaza entrance.

The official Korean-language guide lists fares as KRW 4,000 for adults, KRW 2,000 for teens, and KRW 1,500 for children (circular ticket), with the train closed on Mondays. Hours can shift with weather and season, so double-check on the day of your visit.

What to see at Taejongdae

1. Taejongdae Observatory — the easiest wide-open sea view

One of the Danubi Train’s main stops. From up here you get a broad view of the sea and coastline, and on a very clear day you can spot Tsushima Island, Japan, across the water.

2. Yeongdo Lighthouse — Taejongdae’s defining scene

Yeongdo Lighthouse, first lit in 1906, is Taejongdae’s signature landmark. Around the lighthouse you get cliffs and sea together, and heading down from there leads into the geological scenery toward Sinseon Rock.

3. Sinseon Rock — Taejongdae’s real showstopper

A broad, flat rock shelf below Yeongdo Lighthouse. It’s explained as an uplifted wave-cut platform formed around 120,000 years ago, and it’s one of the clearest, most intuitive reasons Taejongdae is a national geopark.

Safety note

Access around Sinseon Rock can be restricted depending on falling rocks, waves, or weather. Always follow the barriers and signage on site.

"Wait, are those dinosaur footprints?"

If you spot round, footprint-like marks on the rock, what Busan Geopark currently identifies as the notable geological feature here isn’t dinosaur footprints — it’s orbicular hornfels and marine potholes.

Orbicular hornfels is a pattern of concentric rings formed when sedimentary rock in Taejongdae’s coastal cliffs was altered by heat from nearby magma. The smaller rings run about 2–3cm across, the larger ones up to roughly 1m, and the repeating round borders on the rock surface can look like dinosaur footprints at a glance.

A marine pothole is a round hollow formed in a flat wave-cut platform. Pebbles carried in by waves get trapped and spin in place, grinding a round hole into the rock surface over a long time. When several of these sit in a row, they can look like footprints too.

How to make it more fun

If you spot round marks on the ground, check the nearby geological signage before jumping to "dinosaur footprint."

Taejongdae isn’t just scenery — it’s also a fun place to go looking for what waves and geology have carved into the rock.

Walked Taejongdae? Haenyeochon is next

After the cliffs and lighthouse, why not walk over to Taejongdae Haenyeochon for a plate of fresh seafood? You’re eating with the sea right below the cliff, which makes it a natural next stop after the walking route.

Locals in Busan have known this spot for decades — it’s foreign travelers who tend to have never heard of it.

If walking alone leaves you wanting more

There are ways to see Busan sitting down too — the marine cable car crossing the Songdo sea, Busan Tower looking down over downtown Nampo-dong, and a yacht tour looking back up at the coast from the water. Each gives you an angle the walking routes don’t.

The short version

  • First visit: Danubi Train + observatory + Yeongdo Lighthouse is the easiest combination to follow.
  • Picking just one view: Prioritize Yeongdo Lighthouse and the area around Sinseon Rock.
  • If you like walking: Taking the whole loop road slowly is a great option too.
  • Want food too: Taejongdae → Haenyeochon flows naturally.
  • Want a different angle: Consider a sea route too, like the cruise from Jagalchi.
