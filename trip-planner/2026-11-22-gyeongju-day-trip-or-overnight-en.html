Busan to Gyeongju: Day Trip or Overnight? Plus Where to Stay in Gyeongju

Gyeongju is about 30 minutes from Busan by KTX, so many people visit on a day trip. But the sights are spread across three areas, and depending on what you want to see, staying a night can work better.

This guide covers how to choose between a day trip and an overnight stay, and which area to stay in if you spend the night.

As of November 22, 2026 — train and bus times may change.

1. The short answer: which trip suits you

Gyeongju's sights are spread across three areas: downtown, from Hwangnidan-gil to Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond; Bulguksa Temple and Seokguram Grotto on the mountainside to the east; and Bomun Lake Resort around the lake.

On a day trip from Busan, two areas are about as much as you can see comfortably. If you want to see all three, or spend a morning or evening in Gyeongju, stay a night.

3. A day trip from Busan: how to do it

RouteTransportTravel time
Busan Station → Gyeongju Station (KTX)KTXAbout 30 minutes

Haeundae → Gyeongju downtown terminalIntercity bus, every hour at 20 pastAbout 1 hour 15 minutes

If you are staying near Busan Station, Nampo-dong or Seomyeon, you can see the night views and still get back. KTX trains from Gyeongju Station to Busan Station run until after 11 p.m. In the November 2026 timetable, the last train leaves at 11:46 p.m.

Last entry to Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond is 9:30 p.m. Allow a little over 30 minutes by city bus or taxi from there to Gyeongju Station, and you can catch a KTX between 10 and 11 p.m. Late at night, a taxi is the less stressful option.

If you are staying in Haeundae, it's a different story. The last direct intercity bus from Gyeongju to Haeundae leaves at 9:10 p.m., so you'll miss it if you stay for the night views. You'll need to take the KTX to Busan Station and change to the subway.

Subway times from Busan Station to Haeundae are in our guide to Busan's neighborhoods to stay in. You can check the last Line 1 train from Busan Station in the Kakao Metro app. How to check last train times is explained in our guide to Korean map apps.

Suggested day trip order

The easiest order is Bulguksa Temple in the morning, downtown on foot in the afternoon, and Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond around sunset. Bulguksa closes earlier than downtown, and downtown can be enjoyed into the night.

A walking order for downtown is in our Gyeongju downtown walking route, and Bulguksa is covered in our Bulguksa Temple and Seokguram Grotto guide.

Places best left out of a day trip

Seokguram Grotto means changing at Bulguksa to bus 12, which runs about once an hour, so adding it cuts deeply into your time downtown.

Bomun Lake Resort is about 17 minutes from downtown by bus, so you can drop by on a day trip. But it's mainly a place for lakeside walks, so if you have half a day to spend, Bulguksa has more to see.

4. When a night in Gyeongju is worth it

If you want to see Seokguram Grotto
Bus 12 to Seokguram runs about once an hour, and in winter last entry is 5 p.m. Going in the afternoon means rushing, so it's easier to stay a night and go the next morning.

If you want to enjoy the night views at your own pace
Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond, Woljeonggyo Bridge and Cheomseongdae light up after sunset. If your room is nearby, you can stroll without worrying about the last train.

If you want to see Gyeongju in the morning
Bulguksa Temple and Seokguram Grotto open at 9 a.m. If you sleep in Gyeongju, it's easy to arrive at opening time and you get a longer day.

If you want to sleep in a hanok
The lanes around Hwangnidan-gil and Daereungwon are full of small hanok (traditional Korean house) stays. It's a very Gyeongju experience, and reason enough to stay a night.

5. Where to stay in Gyeongju

Around Hwangnidan-gil and Daereungwon: hanok stays and night views

This area has the largest cluster of small hanok stays registered as hanok experience businesses (한옥체험업). Daereungwon, Cheomseongdae and Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond are within walking distance, and the bus terminal is about 10 minutes away on foot.

Buses 10 and 11 to Bulguksa and Bomun Lake Resort leave from right next door, at the Hwangnidan-gil public parking lot.

The downside is that many places are small, with just one room, and big hotels are rare.

Bomun Lake Resort: big lakeside hotels and resorts

Designated in 1975 as Korea's first tourist resort complex. Big hotels, resorts and the Gyeongju World amusement park sit around Bomun Lake. Many rooms are spacious, which suits family trips.

Bus 10 connects it with downtown and Bulguksa, with a stop in front of each hotel and resort.

The downside is getting back after the downtown night views. Bus 10 to Bomun Lake Resort stops running around 9 p.m., so if you come back late, you'll need a taxi.

In front of Bulguksa: early mornings at Bulguksa and Seokguram

The best base for seeing Bulguksa Temple and Seokguram Grotto early in the morning.

The downside is that there are few places to stay, and downtown is about 30 minutes away by bus, a long way back after the night views.

The sea looks much like Busan's

Gyeongju's coast faces the same East Sea as Haeundae, Songjeong and Gijang in Busan. If you only want to see the sea, it won't look very different from what you saw in Busan.

If you've already had plenty of sea in Busan, feel free to skip day 3.

Two things you won't find in Busan

Tomb of King Munmu
A rock in the sea said to be the tomb of King Munmu of Silla, who unified the Three Kingdoms of Korea. The story goes that he asked to be cremated and buried in the East Sea, so that he could become a dragon and guard the kingdom against Japanese raiders coming by sea.

Know before you go: what you see is a rocky islet

There is no rounded earth mound like the royal tombs in downtown Gyeongju. All you see is a single rocky islet in the sea, about 200 m from the beach.

You can't take a boat out to it or look down on it from an observation deck, so you view it from the sand at Bonggil Beach.

Inside the rock, channels let seawater flow in from all four sides, and a large flat stone lies under the water in the middle. People believe the king's remains were buried beneath it, but none of this can be seen from the shore.

So it's a place you visit for the story rather than the view. If the story doesn't interest you, you may be disappointed.

Nearby, Gameunsa Temple Site has the remains of a temple completed by his son, King Sinmun, and two three-story stone pagodas. It's tied to the story of the Tomb of King Munmu, so the two are worth seeing together.

Yangnam Columnar Joints
Rock that cracked into column shapes as lava cooled. The columns fan out toward the sea in an unusual pattern, and there is an observatory on the coastal walking trail.

Getting there

Bus 150 alone takes you to both places. It starts at the bus terminal, passes in front of Hwangnidan-gil (Seorabeol Sageori. Hwangnidan-gil stop, 서라벌사거리.황리단길) and goes through Bomun Lake Resort to the coast. If you're staying at Bomun Lake Resort, you can take the same bus.

The two places are only a little over 10 minutes apart by bus, so they are easy to combine in one day. Bus 150 also goes to Gameunsa Temple Site, three stops before the Tomb of King Munmu (Tapmaeul. Gameunsaji stop, 탑마을.감은사지).

Check the bus times first

Bus 150 runs every 50 minutes to more than 2 hours. The first bus from the bus terminal leaves at 6:40 a.m. and the last at 9 p.m.

Before you get off, check the time of the bus back in a map app. Bus 150 doesn't go to Gampo Port, so seeing it on the same day takes extra time.

7. Check before you book

Bedding at hanok stays
Some hanok stays have you sleep on bedding laid out on the floor, while others have beds. Check the photos to see whether there's a bed.

Your ticket back
On weekends and holidays, KTX trains back to Busan fill up fast. On a day trip, book your return ticket first.

Where you arrive
Trains stop at Gyeongju Station (KTX), away from downtown, while intercity buses stop at the terminal right in the center. If you're staying downtown, the bus can be more convenient.
