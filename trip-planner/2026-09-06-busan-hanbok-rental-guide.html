Where to Rent a Hanbok in Busan

Why It's So Popular — A Trend That Started at Gyeongbokgung

Seoul's four grand palaces (Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung, Deoksugung, Changgyeonggung) waive admission for anyone wearing hanbok. That policy is what kicked off "wear hanbok, get your best shot at the palace" as a huge social media trend — and it's now practically a must-do for international visitors on a Korea trip too. Busan doesn't have a palace, but the same idea spread into "wear hanbok and take photos in a photogenic neighborhood," which is why hanbok rental shops have popped up around spots like Gamcheon Village, Huinnyeoul Village, and Gwangalli.

There's even an official standard for what counts as "real" hanbok

This trend got big enough that the Korea Heritage Service now has an official guideline for the free-admission rule. Under the 2026 standard, only the traditional two-piece form — a jeogori (jacket) paired with a chima (skirt) or baji (pants) — qualifies. One-piece "fusion hanbok" that joins the top and bottom into a single garment doesn't count. That's how far this trend has gone: there's now an official line for what counts as real hanbok.

Wait, Is This Actually Traditional Hanbok?

Look closely at what rental shops actually put you in, though, and it can feel different from the elegant look you remember from period dramas — and that feeling isn't wrong.

Traditional hanbok A wide skirt that falls naturally, tied high above the chest, with a short, simple jacket. Colors are muted and the trim is restrained — closer to what you'd see in a period drama.
Rental-shop (theme) hanbok Exaggerated volume from a puffed-out underskirt, bold pastel or primary-color combinations, and prominent ribbon or lace trim — this is what most rental shops actually stock. It prioritizes how it photographs and how easy it is to put on and take off over historical accuracy.

A Korea Heritage Service official has publicly said that rental-shop hanbok "prioritizes flashy design and easy-care fabric to match what international tourists want." One foreign outlet compared it to "fusion Korean food" — a version that prioritizes accessibility over authenticity. For reference, there's also a middle-ground style called saenghwal hanbok (modernized everyday hanbok), separate from either of these.

So it's easy to picture rental shops as all flashy reinterpretation, but in practice most shops stock both — showy theme hanbok alongside calmer, more traditionally-cut pieces — and let you choose. Ask for the former if you want dramatic photos, or the latter for a more understated look. Either way, the rental process itself is the same.

Comparing the 4 Shops

Shops close or relocate often — the details below were cross-checked as of September 2026, so double-check that a shop is still operating before you book.

Cheolsu & Younghee (철수와 영희)

Right at the entrance to Gamcheon Culture Village, so it doesn't add extra walking to your visit. Rents school uniforms as well as hanbok, with a rooftop photo spot overlooking the village. Popular with international visitors.

Saha-gu, Okcheon-ro 123 · Daily 09:00–17:00

Getting there — From Toseong Station Exit 6, turn right and walk about 120m to the bus stop (Busan National University Hospital). Take village bus Seogu 2-2 or Saha 1-1 and get off at Gamcheon Culture Village.

Ipgo Gagye (입고가게)

Located inside Gamcheon Culture Village, with a wide range of styles from traditional hanbok to fusion and fantasy/cosplay concepts. Most shops in the Gamcheon area lean heavily toward flashy theme hanbok, but this one also keeps a decent stock of traditional pieces. School uniforms available too, plus a rooftop photo spot.

Saha-gu, Gamcheon-dong 2-9 · Daily 10:00–18:00

Getting there — Same as Cheolsu & Younghee (Toseong Station Exit 6 → village bus → get off at Gamcheon Culture Village), then turn right at the information center; it's on the left, about 100m ahead.

Hanminyeo (한미녀)

In the Gwangbok Underground Shopping Arcade near Nampo Station — fits well with the old-downtown (Nampo-dong/Jungang-dong) retro-alley vibe. Besides regular hanbok, it also rents traditional wedding robes like hwarot and wonsam.

Jung-gu, Jungang-daero 17, Gwangbok Underground Arcade A17 · Daily 10:00–18:00 (break 14:00–14:30) · Closed Chuseok

Getting there — From Nampo Station's basement level 1, head toward Exit 7; it's about 40m past the Lotte Department Store entrance.

Noir Hair 2nd Branch (누아르헤어2호점)

This one's in a completely different area — Gwangalli, not the Gamcheon/Nampo-dong cluster. It's primarily a hair salon: book a perm, color, or cut, and a 2-hour hanbok experience comes free with it. That means it doesn't work if you just want to rent hanbok on its own. Worth considering if you're staying near Gwangalli, or want to get your hair done at the same time.

Suyeong-gu, Gwanganhaebyeon-ro 127, 1F (Gwangalli) · Available with a hair-service booking

Getting there — From Geumnyeonsan Station (Busan Metro Line 2) Exit 3, turn around and turn right; about an 8-minute walk.

Which One Fits You

  • Visiting Gamcheon + want an easy, no-fuss pick — Cheolsu & Younghee. Right at the village entrance so there's no extra walking, and school uniforms are available too.
  • Visiting Gamcheon + want a flashy or fantasy concept — Ipgo Gagye. Style range goes from traditional to fusion/fantasy (cosplay).
  • Want the old-downtown retro-alley vibe + a royal-style robe — Hanminyeo (Nampo-dong/Jungang-dong area). Traditional wedding robes (hwarot, wonsam) available too.
  • Staying near Gwangalli + want to get your hair done too — Noir Hair 2nd Branch. The hanbok comes bundled with a hair-service booking, so if you just want a standalone hanbok rental, the other three are the better fit.

Good to Know Before You Book

  • Peak-season booking — During the June–September peak season, booking ahead is safer than walking in same-day.
  • Return time — Even a full-day rental usually has an evening return deadline at most shops. Being late can mean extra charges.
  • Shoes — Shops' loaner shoes often come in limited sizes, so it's safer to wear your own comfortable shoes.
  • Storing your bags — Most shops have free lockers, so you can leave your things and walk around hands-free.
