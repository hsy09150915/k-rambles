Is Busan Tap Water Safe to Drink?

Ask five people and you'll get five different answers — some drink it straight from the tap every day, others swear you should never touch it. Here's what the official numbers say, why people still don't fully trust it, and what actually decides the answer for you.

Checked September 2, 2026

The Nakdong River at Gupo, Busan — the source of the city's tap water, and the river behind the 1991 phenol spill. Photo by 102orion, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons.

What travelers hear online

Split, and unexplained

Ask in a Korea travel forum and the answers contradict each other — some people drink it daily without a thought, others say never, boil everything. Almost nobody explains why, which is exactly the problem.

What the official data says

Safe, by a wide margin

10×stricter than the legal standard
Monthlyexpert-panel testing

Busan's water authority tests city tap water monthly together with a panel of outside experts, and manages it to stay at roughly a tenth of the legal contamination limit. Officially, it's rated safe to drink straight from the tap.

Why the doubt exists at all

A 1991 chemical spill

In 1991, a large amount of phenol from a factory upstream got into the Nakdong River — the same river Busan draws its water from. It flowed into the water supply, and many people were affected. The treatment system is unrelated today, but the distrust it left behind still shapes how locals talk about tap water three decades later.

Okay, but what actually matters for you

If the building looks new, you're basically fine.

Staying somewhere that looks new? Just drink it. Even in an older building, as long as the water doesn't look rusty or smell off, it's almost always fine too — you'd notice if it weren't.

That said, most Koreans run their tap water through a filter anyway. Not because the water itself is unsafe — it's because old pipes can shed tiny amounts of rust you'd never actually see, and a filter catches that before it reaches your glass.

Korean home filters are genuinely good at this part — they're built to strip out rust and sediment, which is exactly why so many people trust filtered water without a second thought.

Bottom line: new building, drink away. Older building with no visible rust or smell, also fine. Still not sure? Do what most locals do and run it through a filter — that's literally what it's for.
