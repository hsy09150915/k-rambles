Busan Trash Rules: When and How to Throw Things Away — 11 Waste Sorting Questions Foreigners Ask Most

Trash has to go out on set days and at set times, in the right bag. Here are 11 questions about waste sorting that foreigners in Busan ask most often.

As of October 2026 — days and times differ by district. Be sure to check the guidance for where you live.

Check this first

Apartment complexes and officetels (오피스텔) often have their own disposal areas or processing facilities inside the complex, and many complexes run on their own rules. So you may not have to follow the district office's complicated day and time rules. When you move in, ask the management office (관리사무소) first. They will explain how the complex handles general waste, food waste, recyclables, and even bulky waste (대형폐기물).

This guide is the district-office guidance for people who live in villas (빌라), multi-household houses, and detached houses, which have no disposal area or rules of their own.

At a glance

In Korea, you can't throw trash away at any time or in any bag. This guide picks 11 questions that foreigners living in Busan ask often and sums up what you need to follow. Days and times differ by district, so it is based on district-office guidance for Busan's 15 districts and Gijang-gun (기장군, a county): Dong-gu (동구), Seo-gu (서구), Nam-gu (남구), Buk-gu (북구), Dongnae-gu (동래구), Suyeong-gu (수영구), Yeonje-gu (연제구), Busanjin-gu (부산진구), Geumjeong-gu (금정구), Yeongdo-gu (영도구), Gangseo-gu (강서구), Saha-gu (사하구), Sasang-gu (사상구), Jung-gu (중구), Haeundae-gu (해운대구), and Gijang-gun (기장군).

1. Can I throw trash away at any time?

No. In Korea you can put trash out only on set days and at set times. You must not leave it in front of your home or building outside those times. This is what surprises newly arrived foreigners the most. Some people thought they could throw trash away whenever they liked and later said it was "a big misunderstanding."

In Busan, general waste and food waste are usually put out on Sundays, Tuesdays, and Thursdays in front of your front gate, or at the ground-floor entrance of a shop. Almost every district uses the same days and places, and you can't put trash out on Fridays and Saturdays. What differs is the time. 2. Can I use any trash bag?

No. General waste has to go in the designated volume-based standard bag (종량제봉투). If you put it out in an ordinary plastic bag, it may not be collected. The Dong-gu (동구) guidance also says that putting trash out without a standard bag can lead to refused collection and a fine (과태료).

You can buy volume-based waste bags (종량제봉투) at convenience stores and supermarkets, and the price depends on the size. Prices may differ by district, so it's a good idea to start with a small size.

3. How do I throw away food waste?

Food waste is thrown away separately from general waste. In detached houses and villas (빌라), you put it out in a dedicated bin (전용 용기), not a bag, and each time you must attach a payment tag (납부필증), a plastic "chip," to the bin. Without a chip, it won't be collected. You pay a fee based on how much food waste you throw away, which is the same idea as buying food-waste volume-based bags in some Seoul districts. The difference is that you use a bin instead of a bag, and you pay the fee with a chip.

When do I put it out? Food waste also goes out on the same Sundays, Tuesdays, and Thursdays as general waste, in front of your gate or at the ground-floor entrance, during the evening hours set by your district. You can't put it out on Fridays and Saturdays. See the table in question 1 above for each district's hours.

When you move in, ask the real estate agent (부동산) or your landlord first. They can tell you fastest whether the home already has a dedicated bin and where to buy one. The steps to get ready are as follows.

  1. Buy a dedicated bin (전용 용기). They are sold at stores designated by the district office, such as neighborhood supermarkets. For homes, there are 3L and 5L sizes. They are also sold on online shopping sites like Coupang, but check that the bin is the right one for your district before you buy. You buy a bin once and keep using it.
  2. Buy payment tags (납부필증, "chips"). The chip is the fee you pay each time you throw away food waste. Buy several at once from a district-designated seller, such as a volume-based waste bag (종량제봉투) store or a Saemaeul Geumgo (새마을금고) branch, and use one each time.
  3. Fill the bin and attach a chip. Put one chip that matches the bin size into the tag holder (꽂이) near the lid of the bin.
  4. Put it out in front of your gate. The collector removes the chip and empties the food waste. Bring the empty bin back inside and store it.

Here is what the dedicated bin and the chips look like. The lid shows the put-out time and place, and you insert the chip into the holder next to the lid. Chips come in a different color for each bin size, and colors and shapes may differ by district.

Try this in summer

In hot weather, food waste spoils quickly and fruit flies appear. First, drain as much water as you can. Leave it in a strainer, or wrap it in paper towels or newspaper and squeeze it well, and the smell and weight go down. Then put small amounts in sealed containers or bags and freeze them until put-out day, which keeps down smells and bugs. When you put it in the freezer, seal it tightly so it doesn't touch other food, and keep it together in one separate section. A TV investigation also found that bacteria grew in freezers where food waste was frozen, so don't keep it long. Empty it out in time for put-out day.

4. Are chicken bones and onion skins food waste too?

This is what foreigners get confused about most. Not everything you eat counts as food waste. The rule is that things that can be used as animal feed are food waste, and things that can't (bones, hard shells, dregs, and so on) are general waste (according to Yeonje-gu (연제구) guidance). It may differ slightly by district.

Where do I put non-combustible (불연성) trash? Unlike the general waste volume-based waste bag (종량제봉투), there is a separate dedicated non-combustible sack (불연성 마대). It is sold together with volume-based waste bags at bag sellers. For a small amount, such as a little broken glass (under 30L), some districts let you wrap it in newspaper and put it in a volume-based waste bag (종량제봉투). This is the case in Busanjin-gu (부산진구), Geumjeong-gu (금정구), and Buk-gu (북구).

If combustible and non-combustible trash are mixed, it won't be collected.

Try this

Sharp things like broken glass should be wrapped thickly in newspaper or similar so nobody gets hurt, and put out the way your district sets for non-combustible trash (a dedicated sack or a volume-based waste bag). Dong-gu (동구) says to use the 20L non-combustible waste sack (불연성 전용마대).

7. How are apartments and detached houses different?

Apartments (아파트) are often run by the complex with its own times and methods. There may be a separate sorting area, and sometimes separate days are set.

Villas (빌라) put trash out in front of the building on the days and at the times set by the district office (구청), the same as detached houses. Some buildings with many households have set up a trash spot or recycling bins, so first look at the notices near the entrance and the parking lot.

Officetels (오피스텔) also often run their own disposal spots and recycling bins, like apartments. They are sometimes at a designated spot outside the building or in the basement. Check the location, days, and times with the management office (관리사무소) or the notices.

Detached houses put trash out in front of the gate or at a set place, on the days and at the times set by the district office (구청).

Try this

Rules differ by housing type even within the same district, so it also helps to watch once how your neighbors put things out. If you can't find where to put trash, look outside the building, in the basement, or around the parking lot, and if you still can't tell, ask the management office (관리사무소) or your landlord. If you're still looking for a home, ask the real estate agent (부동산) too, before signing, where and when to put trash out.

8. How do I throw away big items?

Big items such as beds, sofas, and desks can't be put out as general waste. You must first report them to the district office (구청) as bulky waste (대형폐기물) and put them out the way you are told. In many districts you can report by phone, on the internet, or through an app.

In apartment complexes and other multi-unit housing (공동주택), ask the management office (관리사무소) first. In many apartments, the management office reports and handles big items for you. In those places you don't have to report to the district office (구청) yourself.

For large used appliances such as refrigerators, washing machines, air conditioners, and TVs, the seller may take the old one away for free when you buy a new one, and you can also apply for the free home-visit pickup service. Reserve in advance through the call center at 1599-0903 (weekdays 08:00–18:00) or online at www.15990903.or.kr. If you have 5 or more small used appliances, you can apply for them together with the large ones.

For small used appliances such as rice cookers, vacuum cleaners, fans, laptops, and hair dryers, multi-unit housing (공동주택) has dedicated bins inside the complex that you can use at any time; villas (빌라) and detached houses without a bin put them in a clear bag and out on recycling day. Small used appliances under 1m go out on Wednesdays in Dong-gu (동구). Always remove the battery from a mobile phone and put it separately in a dedicated bin.

Electric blankets, jade mats, lighting fixtures, musical instruments, electric massage chairs, and furniture such as wardrobes, beds, and mattresses can't be recycled. Contact the cleaning department of your district, apply separately to a bulky waste (대형폐기물) collection company, and pay a disposal fee separately.

9. What if I miss the put-out day?

Don't worry. Just keep it at home until the next put-out day and then put it out. The problem is putting trash outside on a non-put-out day or during a forbidden time.

Smelly food waste is easier to handle if you freeze it or keep it sealed. Also keep in mind that trash can pile up over the weekend, because nothing can be put out on Fridays and Saturdays.

10. What happens if I don't follow the rules?

District guidance sets out the cases where collection is refused or a fine (과태료) is charged. In the Dong-gu (동구) guidance, these include not using a standard bag, not using a dedicated bin, putting trash out without sorting it, breaking the set time, place, or day, and illegal dumping or burning.

Yeonje-gu (연제구) says it won't collect food waste that hasn't been drained or has residue in it, or food waste without a payment tag (납부필증) attached. The Haeundae-gu (해운대구), Gangseo-gu (강서구), and Saha-gu (사하구) guidance says that putting trash out without a standard bag, or illegal dumping or burning, carries a fine (과태료) of up to 1 million won. Saha-gu (사하구) says it won't collect if recyclables and general waste are mixed, if recyclables aren't sorted by item, or if food waste isn't drained. Gangseo-gu (강서구) also says it refuses collection if food waste is mixed with general waste. Amounts may differ in other districts, so check the guidance for your district.

Try this

If uncollected trash is left, find out why, sort it again, and put it out on the next put-out day. If you don't know, ask the management office (관리사무소).

11. What should I do first right after moving in?

  • Ask your landlord, the management office (관리사무소), or the community center (행정복지센터) about the put-out days and times.
  • Ask where to buy standard bags and a food-waste bin.
  • Take a photo of the recycling schedule.
  • Before throwing away a big item, check how to report it.
