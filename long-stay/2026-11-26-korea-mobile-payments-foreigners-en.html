Mobile Payments in Korea for Foreigners: Korean Pay Apps and Your Home Wallet App

In Korea, people pay with phone payment apps as well as cards. But most Korean pay apps need a Korean phone number and an Alien Registration Card (ARC, 외국인등록증) to sign up.

That doesn't mean foreigners can't pay by phone. This guide covers how to pay at Korean shops with the card or home-country wallet app you already have, and which Korean pay apps you can use with just a passport.

As of November 26, 2026 — payment services change often.

2. Tap to pay with your phone: which shops and what share

Koreans have held their phones to card terminals to pay for years. That's because Samsung Pay, launched in 2015, used a magnetic method (MST) that works on ordinary card terminals without any upgrade.

Foreign cards and Apple Pay, however, pay through NFC (EMV contactless), the international standard. Since Samsung Pay worked fine on existing terminals, shops had little reason to add NFC terminals, and even at the end of 2025 only about 10% of shops were estimated to have one.

That's why, at many shops, the same-looking tap worked for Koreans' phones but not for foreigners' cards and phones. In this guide, "tap payment" means this NFC method.

You don't need to worry about a physical foreign card. It has an IC chip and a magnetic stripe, so at any shop that accepts foreign cards you can insert or swipe it even if tapping doesn't work. You really need tap payment only when paying with a phone or watch, as with Apple Pay.

Recently Toss (토스) and Naver Pay (네이버페이) have been rolling out new payment terminals to shops quickly, and that is changing things. Both terminals support NFC, so you can tap a foreign card or Apple Pay on them.

Which shops take it?

Whether tap payment works depends much more on the type of shop than on how the terminal looks. Below is the share of Korean card-accepting shops that take Apple Pay. Tapping a foreign card needs the same NFC terminal, so the figures should be roughly the same for cards.

Type of shopShare that takes tap payment
Convenience stores, cafés, big supermarketsOver 60%
Small shopsAbout 6%
All card-accepting shopsAbout 25%

Figures are for the first quarter of 2026. Since then, new terminals have kept spreading fast: Toss alone added its terminal at 200,000 more shops in six months.

Convenience stores, big supermarkets and large chains take tap payment on the flat card terminal at the counter too. You don't need a Toss or Naver terminal for it to work.

Toss and Naver terminals are spreading mainly in independent cafés and restaurants. Together they are in about 19% of all card-accepting shops in Korea, and they are more common in neighborhoods popular with young people; in the neighborhood with the highest share, 1 in 3 shops uses a Toss terminal.

On the other hand, many older restaurants and small market stalls still don't take tap payment.

How to tell if a shop takes it

Tap payment symbol
Looks like a Wi-Fi symbol turned on its side

Look for the tap payment symbol above, or the Apple Pay logo (an apple with "Pay" next to it), on the terminal or the counter. If the back of your foreign card has the same curved-line symbol, that card can be used for tap payment.

If you're not sure, show your phone at the counter and ask "Teochi dwaeyo? (터치 돼요?)", meaning "Can I tap?", and the staff will tell you.

If paying by phone doesn't work

Take out your physical card and insert it, and it will usually go through. Don't rely on Apple Pay alone; always carry a physical foreign card.

For why foreign cards work at some shops and not others, see our practical guide to paying with a foreign card.

3. Paying with your home-country wallet app

Many shops let you pay straight from the wallet app you use at home, without installing anything in Korea. The amount is usually charged in your home currency, so you don't need to exchange money.

How does it connect?

There is a payment network called Alipay+ that puts wallet apps from many countries behind a single QR. The name is close to Alipay, but it isn't the Alipay app itself; it's a network linking apps from many countries.

In Korea, this network is linked to ZeroPay (제로페이) and to Kakao Pay (카카오페이) merchants. ZeroPay is Korea's public QR payment system made for small shops, and it is connected to 76 overseas payment apps from 24 countries.

Steps to pay

Either scan the shop's QR with your app and enter the amount, or show the payment code from your app to the staff. Which one depends on the shop, so show your app screen at the counter and say "QR", and the staff will guide you.

In the MoMo app, open the QR scan screen, choose Korea as the country, then scan the shop's QR with the Alipay+ logo. Enter the amount in Korean won and choose how to pay; the amount converted into Vietnamese dong appears, and tapping confirm completes the payment.

Do this before you come to Korea

Overseas payments often work only on accounts that have finished identity verification and bank linking in the app. MoMo, too, requires you to link a bank account and verify with your chip-based national ID card before you can pay abroad.

Doing this after you arrive may require a verification code sent by SMS to your home-country number, so before you leave, make sure overseas payment is turned on in your app.

4. If you want to use Korean pay apps yourself

The three pay apps Koreans use most are Toss, Naver Pay and Kakao Pay. What you can do without an ARC differs from app to app.

AppWithout an ARCWhat you can use
Toss appSign up with a passportSome features, such as prepaid top-ups, transfers and prepaid card payments
Naver Pay appDifficultNeeds real-name verification with a Korean number, and foreign cards can't be registered
Kakao Pay appDifficultNeeds a Korean number, an ARC and a Korean bank account

Signing up for the Toss app with a passport

The Toss app can be used in 10 languages, including English, Chinese and Vietnamese. Here's how to sign up.

App Store Google Play
  1. Install and open the Toss app.
  2. Choose your country and whether you are a Korean national.
  3. Take a photo of your physical passport, which must still be valid.
  4. Set a password and enter an email address for confirmation, and you're done.

Later, once you have an ARC and a Korean number, you can sign up again with your phone number and use every feature.

Paying with Toss prepaid

Toss runs a prepaid service for foreign visitors that you can open with your passport details, without an ARC. You can spend the money through the prepaid card or Toss Pay (토스페이) at Korean shops online and offline, for things like accommodation, food, shopping and transport.

You can top up with cash at a convenience store, and before you leave Korea, you can get a refund of any remaining balance. You can also pay at online shops that accept Toss Pay.

However, if a shopping or delivery app itself asks for Korean phone identity verification when you sign up, you may get stuck at sign-up before you ever reach payment.

There is a top-up limit, so it isn't meant for holding large sums. Check the limit in the app.

Naver and Kakao

The Naver Pay app still requires real-name verification with a Korean number.

Instead, when you book or order at a shop in the Naver Map (네이버 지도) app, you can verify with your passport (available since June 2026) and pay with a foreign card.

However, this only works at shops that have chosen to accept foreign cards, and not many have done so yet. At shops that haven't, you'll get stuck at the payment step if you try to pay a deposit or pay for an order in the Naver Map app.

This applies only to paying in advance inside the Naver Map app. At the same shop, paying with a foreign card at the counter in person usually works. If in-app payment is blocked, pick a shop that takes bookings without payment, or just walk in and pay there.

You can sign up for KakaoTalk (카카오톡) with an overseas number, but the Kakao Pay transfers and payments inside it need a Korean number and an ARC. See our guide to getting a Korean phone line for details.
