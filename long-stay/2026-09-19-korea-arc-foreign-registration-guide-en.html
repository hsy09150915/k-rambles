Do I Need a Korean Alien Registration Card (ARC)?

Under 90 days, you’re not even eligible — 91 days and up, it’s mandatory. It’s the same 90-day line you may have already hit shopping for a SIM card.

As of September 2026 — related rules and document requirements can change without notice.

A sample Alien Registration Card — this is what it looks like. The name and number shown are example data.

If you’re planning to stay in Korea for about 3 months, you’ve probably wondered: "do I actually need to register as a foreign resident?"

You’ve actually run into this exact line before, if you shopped for a SIM card — prepaid SIMs top out at 90 days, full stop. Stay 91 days or more, and you’ll need both a postpaid plan and the registration card. It’s the same cutoff.

Why 91 days is the line

Korea’s Immigration Act requires foreign registration for anyone staying 91 days or more from the date of entry.

Every "tourist" route into Korea caps out at 90 days, no matter which one you use — a short-visit (C-3) entry is discretionary up to 90 days for the immigration officer, and visa-free entry runs 30 to 90 days depending on your country. So "tourist" and "registration-required" structurally never overlap — anyone staying 91+ days didn’t come in on a tourist visa in the first place; they came on a separate long-term visa for study, work, or marriage.

  • Up to 90 days: you’re not even eligible to register — they won’t take an application even if you want to.
  • 91 days and up: it’s mandatory. Fail to register within 90 days of entry and a fine follows.

Tourist visa (C-3) or short visa-free visits don’t apply here — if you hold a long-term visa like study (D-2/D-4), employment (E-series), or marriage migration (F-6), you’re almost certainly required to register.

How to apply

You can walk in without a booking, but really, book ahead

For major business like foreign registration, appointment holders are served first as a rule. Walking in without a booking is often still accepted, but you get bumped behind everyone with a reservation, so the wait can run far longer.

You can’t book a same-day slot, so don’t wait until your deadline is close — reserve with plenty of room to spare.

Documents needed

  • Passport (photo page + visa page copies)
  • The integrated application form
  • One passport photo (3.5×4.5cm)
  • Proof of address (a lease, a letter from whoever is housing you, a utility bill, etc. — one of these)
Didn’t bring a passport photo?

Subway stations usually have an unmanned ID-photo booth (about KRW 7,000–8,000), and searching "사진관" (photo studio) on Naver Map also turns up results.

Those search results mix in photo-booth chains like Life Four Cuts, which don’t produce ID-spec photos — check Street View for an old-school studio storefront first to avoid a wasted trip.

Every ARC issued since January 2025 comes with a built-in electronic chip (IC) automatically — it’s not something you request or opt into, it applies to everyone getting a card issued for the first time right now. Just tap your card against your phone in the mobile ID app, and the mobile version is issued instantly.

Staying 3 months in a hotel?

It’s often easier than it sounds.

A hotel or Airbnb payment receipt (with your name, the address, and the length of stay printed on it) or a letter from your host confirming they’re putting you up will often work as proof of address. It’s not asking for anything elaborate — just something showing this person stayed at this address for this period.

Front desk staff aren’t always used to producing this kind of document, though, so it’s worth asking at check-in whether they can issue a "stay confirmation letter for immigration purposes."

One variable left — your day count

If it’s 91 days or more, go ahead and register using the method above; if it’s 90 or under, you’re not eligible to register in the first place.

The SIM card connection

Prepaid SIMs cap out at 90 days across the board — SKT, KT, and third-party resellers like Trazy and Holafly alike, since they’re all reselling the same underlying carrier product with no way around the limit.

Past 91 days, switching to a postpaid plan is the only path forward, and the ARC is all you need to sign up.
