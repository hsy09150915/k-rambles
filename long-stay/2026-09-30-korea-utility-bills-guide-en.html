Korea's Maintenance Fees (Gwallibi) & Utility Bills for Foreigners

What every unfamiliar item on a 관리비 (gwallibi, building maintenance fee) bill means, and where to pay electricity, gas and water.

As of September 2026

1. What is 관리비 (gwallibi)?

Living in Korea, you'll get a monthly bill called 관리비 (gwallibi, "building maintenance fee") every month. Electricity and water are usually included in this one bill — only gas comes as a separate bill, and only in a building heated by an individual gas boiler (개별난방). At first it's easy to get confused about what's bundled and what isn't.

Korea has a high share of shared apartment-style housing (공동주택, "multi-unit housing") — think apartments and officetels where many households share one building. So the building-wide management cost is split per household, and each unit's own electricity and water usage is broken out too, all on one combined bill. The structure behind it comes down to two parts.

PartWhat it is
공용 (gongyong, "shared")The cost of things the whole building uses together (security, cleaning, disinfection, elevator upkeep, etc.), split across units by each unit's share of the building's floor area
개별 (gaebyeol, "individual")Charged strictly by what each unit actually used. Electricity and water are billed as line items inside this same gwallibi bill. Gas depends on the building's heating type — with 지역난방 (jiyeok-nanbang, "district heating"), heating/hot-water charges (난방비/급탕비) are folded straight into the gwallibi bill with no separate bill at all; with 개별난방 (gaebyeol-nanbang, "individual heating" — a gas boiler in the unit), gas is billed entirely separately by the city gas company, independent of gwallibi. (For a long-term lease, just ask your agent at signing whether the building uses 지역난방 or 개별난방 — that tells you instantly whether a separate gas bill is coming.)

The gwallibi bill is dropped straight into your mailbox every month by the building's management office (관리사무소, gwalli-samuso). It does not get withdrawn automatically unless you've set that up — you need to wire the payment yourself to the management office's bank account printed on the bill. To set up autopay, you don't apply at the management office itself — you apply at whichever bank the management office uses. Ask the management office which bank that is first, then register through that bank's app or a branch using the electronic payment number (전자납부번호) printed on the bill.

A gwallibi bill has these three parts

Sample bill format (company name and amounts shown are fictional examples)

PartWhat it shows
① Charge summaryThis month's charge (당월부과액, dangwol-bugwa-aek), any overdue balance plus late fee (미납액·미납연체료) if applicable, and at the bottom, two amounts side by side: 납기내금액 (napgi-nae-geumaek, "amount due within the deadline") and 납기후금액 (napgi-hu-geumaek, "amount due after the deadline," which is higher because a late fee has been added). Check the date carefully and always pay the 납기내금액 before the deadline. If you miss the deadline and still pay only the 납기내금액, the shortfall (the late fee) gets added to next month's gwallibi bill.
② 사용량별 지침 (sayongnyang-byeol jichim, "usage details")Shows, for electricity, hot water, water and heating (and cooling, in some buildings), both how much you used and the resulting charge for the month. It compares this month's usage against last month's.
③ 관리비 납입영수증 (gwallibi nabip-yeongsujeung, "itemized receipt")Converts the usage in ② into actual won amounts, broken down item by item. We'll walk through a real example just below.

The breakdown differs by building type

Building typeHow gwallibi is structured
Apartments (아파트) · Officetels (오피스텔)Both are 공동주택-style shared housing, so the basic structure is the same — 공용 + 개별 + the long-term repair reserve fund all combine into one bill, and electricity/gas/water are usually folded into gwallibi. An 오피스텔 (officetel, a mixed office/residential building) sometimes has different item breakdowns since some units are used as offices.
Villa-style low-rise buildings (빌라)Sometimes gwallibi is bundled into the monthly rent, sometimes it's charged separately. In Seoul, even a 빌라 often has a management company; a small building with no management company often just folds internet and a basic maintenance fee into the rent (add-ons like IPTV are usually separate), while electricity and gas are still billed separately by individual meter. This varies a lot building to building, so always confirm before signing.

Items marked "mostly seen in large apartment complexes" above (대표회운영비, 무인택배유지, 중앙정수유지, 커뮤니티운영) may or may not exist depending on the building. The example above is a large apartment complex with many line items — an officetel or a small building is much simpler, typically just 시설관리도급비, 청소용역비, 수선유지비, 관리단운영비, 전기안전대행료, 일반관리비, 보험료 and 제세공과금. Names vary slightly, but the underlying logic — "breaking the shared portion down into pieces" — is the same everywhere.

Long-term repair reserve fund (장기수선충당금) — always check this when you move out

장기수선충당금 (jangi-suseon-chungdanggeum) is money set aside in advance for future major repairs (repainting the exterior, replacing the elevator, etc.). In principle it's the landlord's expense, but the management office collects it from tenants every month along with gwallibi as a matter of convenience.

Whatever you've paid over your lease, you can get it back from your landlord when you move out. Rather than contacting the management office yourself, it's easier to ask the real estate agent who handled your lease to arrange the refund — the agent will confirm the amount with the management office and pass it on to the landlord. Plenty of tenants never claim this, so make sure you do.

2. Electricity — KEPCO (한전, Korea Electric Power Corporation)

A single company, 한전 (KEPCO), supplies electricity everywhere in Korea.

  • When you move in, you need to do a 명의변경 (myeong-ui-byeon-gyeong, "name transfer") to register yourself as the user. Your power won't get cut off if you skip this, but the bill keeps going out under the previous tenant's name, which can cause problems later.
  • Both 명의변경 and autopay can be set up through 한전ON (website + app). 한전ON is Korean-only, though — no official English version was found. Even so, it may still beat a phone call: you can feed the Korean text on screen through a translation app (like Google Translate's camera mode) line by line, which you obviously can't do on a phone call.
  • The call center is 123 (no area code needed), but it's a Korean-language line by default — no dedicated English line was confirmed. If you really need to call, it's worth pairing it with an interpretation service like BBB Korea (volunteer phone interpretation, 1588-5644).
  • Setting up autopay from your bank account means you don't have to pay it manually every month.

3. City gas — Busan uses 부산도시가스

Here's something worth knowing: city gas isn't one nationwide company — the provider is different region by region. Busan is supplied by 부산도시가스 (Busan City Gas). (Seoul, for reference, uses a different company such as Daesung Energy.)

  • You can file a "move-in application (전입 신청)" when you arrive and a "move-out application (전출 신청)" when you leave, both online on their website. There's also an English (ENG) version of the site, but it's unclear whether the actual move-in/move-out processing works fully in English — if not, pair it with a translation app or the interpretation service below.
  • Bills go out by KakaoTalk notification, text message, or mail 7 days before the payment deadline.
  • The customer center is 1544-0009, and it's also unclear whether English-speaking agents are available. If a call is unavoidable, pairing it with an interpretation service like BBB Korea (1588-5644) is worth doing.

Most homes do get a gas bill

Individual gas boiler heating (개별난방) is still the most common setup in Korean housing. In that case, both cooking (gas stove) and heating/hot water run on city gas, so a gas bill is naturally charged.

The exception is a newer officetel or apartment on 지역난방 (district heating, where heating/hot water is already folded into the gwallibi bill) that also uses an induction or electric cooktop — with no gas meter at all in that case, you may simply never get a separate city gas bill.

4. Water — Busan Metropolitan City Waterworks Authority

Water isn't run by a private company — it's managed directly by the local government (Busan City).

  • In most apartments and officetels, the water charge is already folded into gwallibi as an individual usage line item, so there's nothing separate to apply for.
  • In a 빌라, water often comes as its own separate bill, in which case you may need to do a 명의변경 (name transfer) there too.

Check things in this order

  1. 1

    Find out the building type when you sign the lease

    Do this at the lease-signing stage, not after you've already moved in. An officetel is likely to have electricity and water folded into gwallibi; gas, if the building uses 개별난방 (individual heating), will arrive as its own separate bill in your mailbox regardless of gwallibi. Just ask your agent directly at signing: "What's included in the gwallibi?"

  2. 2

    Do a name transfer for anything billed separately from gwallibi

    If any of electricity, gas or water isn't folded into gwallibi and instead arrives as its own bill, do a 명의변경 (name transfer) with 한전ON, 부산도시가스, or the waterworks authority — whichever applies.

  3. 3

    Set up autopay where you can

    Bank autopay means you never have to remember to pay it manually each month.

  4. 4

    Claim the long-term repair reserve fund when you move out

    Ask the real estate agent who handled your lease to arrange the refund. The agent confirms the amount with the management office and passes it on to your landlord. Don't skip this — if you don't ask, you won't get it back.

This article is for general information — the exact items and amounts vary by building, region, and time. For anything specific to your own unit, confirm directly with the management office and each utility provider.
